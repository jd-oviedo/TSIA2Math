#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Percent Means "Out of 100"

That is not a definition to memorize. It is a conversion instruction.

$25\%$ means $\frac{25}{100}$, which means $0.25$. Every percent you will ever meet becomes a decimal by moving the point **two** places left, and every percent problem gets easier the moment you do it.

- $25\% \rightarrow 0.25$
- $8\% \rightarrow 0.08$
- $150\% \rightarrow 1.50$
- $0.5\% \rightarrow 0.005$

Two places. Not one, not three. Moving one place turns $15\%$ into $1.5$ and multiplies your answer by ten, which is the most common arithmetic slip in this topic and the easiest to catch: if your answer to "$15\%$ of 240" is bigger than 240, you moved the wrong number of places.

**Convert first, then compute.** Do not try to reason about a percent while it still has a percent sign on it.

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

Percent change is measured against the **original** value. Always the original. Never the new one.

A price rises from \$50 to \$65. What is the percent increase?

The change is $65 - 50 = 15$. Now: 15 out of what?

Out of **50**, the price before the change. $\frac{15}{50} = 0.30 = 30\%$.

The trap is dividing by 65, the new price, which gives $\frac{15}{65} \approx 23.1\%$. It looks like a reasonable answer and it is the answer to a different question.

Here is the way to keep it straight. A percent change tells you how much the *starting* value grew. You cannot measure growth against the size something ended up being, because the ending size already contains the growth. The original is the only fixed reference point.

$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}}$$

There is a second version of this same error worth naming: reporting $\frac{65}{50} = 130\%$. That is a real quantity, the new price *as a percent of* the old one, and it is not the change. $130\%$ of the original means a $30\%$ increase. The change is the part above $100\%$.

---

##### The Three Percent Questions

Almost everything in this topic is one of three shapes. Learn to tell them apart by what is missing.

**Shape 1: find the part.** "What is $15\%$ of 240?"  
Convert and multiply. $0.15 \times 240 = 36$.

**Shape 2: find the percent.** "18 is what percent of 120?"  
Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. $\frac{18}{120} = 0.15 = 15\%$.

The two traps here are opposite. Forgetting the $\times 100$ leaves you writing "$0.15\%$", which is a real number a thousand times too small. Dividing the wrong way gives $\frac{120}{18} \approx 6.67$, which as a percent is $667\%$ and should be obviously wrong: 18 is smaller than 120, so the answer has to be under $100\%$.

**Shape 3: find the whole.** "18 is $15\%$ of what?"  
Divide the part by the decimal. $18 \div 0.15 = 120$.

**Sanity rule for all three: the part is smaller than the whole unless the percent is over 100.** Check your answer against that before you move on.

---

##### Discounts and Markups

**Example 1:** A shirt costs \$40 and is marked down $25\%$. What is the sale price?

The two-step way: the discount is $0.25 \times 40 = 10$, so the price is $40 - 10 = 30$.

The one-step way: paying $25\%$ less means paying $75\%$, so $0.75 \times 40 = 30$.

The one-step way is faster and it protects you from the single most common error in this section, which is **answering with the discount instead of the price**. \$10 is the amount you saved. \$30 is what you hand over. The question almost always wants the price.

Same idea upward. A $25\%$ markup means paying $125\%$, so multiply by $1.25$.

- Down $p\%$: multiply by $1 - p$ as a decimal.
- Up $p\%$: multiply by $1 + p$ as a decimal.

---

##### Percents Do Not Add

This is the idea that separates Proficient from Basic, and it is genuinely surprising the first time.

**Example 2:** A price is increased by $30\%$ and then decreased by $30\%$. Where does it end up?

Almost everyone says: back where it started. It does not.

Start at \$100.

- Up $30\%$: $100 \times 1.30 = 130$.
- Down $30\%$: $130 \times 0.70 = 91$.

You end at \$91, which is $9\%$ **below** where you began.

Why? The $30\%$ increase was $30\%$ of 100, worth \$30. The $30\%$ decrease was $30\%$ of **130**, worth \$39. The second percent was applied to a bigger number, so it moved more. The percents were equal; the amounts were not.

**Successive percents multiply, they do not add.** $1.30 \times 0.70 = 0.91$, and that $0.91$ is the whole story.

**Example 3:** A jacket costs \$80. The price rises $25\%$, then that new price is discounted $20\%$.

- $80 \times 1.25 = 100$
- $100 \times 0.80 = 80$

Back to exactly \$80. Here two *unequal* percents cancelled perfectly, because $1.25 \times 0.80 = 1$. Adding would have told you $+5\%$, which is wrong.

If you remember one thing: **you cannot add percent changes together.** Multiply the factors.

---

##### Reading the Question's Base

**Example 4:** A bill is \$60. An $8\%$ tax is added, then a $20\%$ tip is calculated on the taxed amount.

Each percent has its own base, and the question tells you which.

- Tax: $60 \times 1.08 = 64.80$
- Tip: $0.20 \times 64.80 = 12.96$, because the question said the tip is on the taxed amount
- Total: $64.80 + 12.96 = 77.76$

Had the tip been calculated on the pre-tax bill, it would be $0.20 \times 60 = 12$ and the total \$76.80. Both are correct arithmetic. Only one answers the question asked.

**Underline the base before you multiply.** When a problem says "of the new price" or "on the original", that phrase is the whole item.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Dividing by the new value.** Percent change is always over the original.
2. **Answering with the discount.** \$10 off a \$40 shirt makes the price \$30, not \$10.
3. **Adding successive percents.** Up $30\%$ then down $30\%$ is down $9\%$, not back to even.
4. **Moving the decimal one place.** $15\%$ is $0.15$, not $1.5$. If the part came out bigger than the whole, this is why.

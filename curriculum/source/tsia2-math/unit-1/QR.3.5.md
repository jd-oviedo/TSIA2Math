#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Counting Things That Match

If someone hands you 3 quarters and then 5 more quarters, you have 8 quarters. You did not have to think about it. You counted the things that matched.

That is combining like terms. The whole topic is counting things that match and refusing to count things that do not.

$$3x + 5x = 8x$$

Read that as "3 x's plus 5 x's is 8 x's." You are not solving for anything. There is no equals sign in the original problem and no value of $x$ to find. You are just rewriting the expression in a shorter form that means exactly the same thing. That is what **simplify** means on this test: same value, fewer pieces.

---

##### Terms, Coefficients, and Variables

Three words, and you need all three.

A **term** is one chunk of an expression, separated from its neighbors by a plus or minus sign. In $4x + 7 - 2y$, there are three terms: $4x$, $7$, and $-2y$.

The **coefficient** is the number multiplying the variable. In $4x$, the coefficient is $4$. In $-2y$, the coefficient is $-2$. The minus sign belongs to the term, not to the space between terms. Hold onto that. It is worth points later.

The **variable** is the letter. In $4x$, the variable is $x$.

A term with no letter at all, like the $7$ above, is a **constant**. Its value never changes no matter what $x$ turns out to be.

---

##### Like Terms Travel Together

**Like terms** are terms with the exact same variable part.

- $4x$ and $9x$ are like terms. Same letter.
- $3y$ and $-8y$ are like terms. Same letter.
- $6$ and $11$ are like terms. Both constants.
- $4x$ and $4y$ are **not** like terms. Different letters.
- $5x$ and $5$ are **not** like terms. One has a variable, one does not.

Only like terms can be combined. This is not a rule someone invented to make your life harder. Go back to the quarters. Three quarters plus five quarters is eight quarters. But three quarters plus five dimes is not eight of anything. There is no single coin that answers it. You have to report both: three quarters and five dimes.

So $3x + 5y$ is already as simple as it gets. If a choice on the test collapses it into $8xy$ or $8x$, that choice is wrong.

---

##### Add the Coefficients, Keep the Variable

Here is the mechanical rule.

**To combine like terms, add or subtract the coefficients. Leave the variable exactly as it was.**

**Example 1:** Simplify $3x + 2x$.

- Both terms are $x$ terms, so they combine.
- Add the coefficients: $3 + 2 = 5$.
- Keep the variable: $5x$.

Two things the variable does **not** do here, and both show up as wrong answers:

- The variable does not multiply. $3x + 2x$ is $5x$, not $5x^{2}$. You are counting x's, not multiplying them.
- The variable does not disappear. $3x + 2x$ is $5x$, not $5$. Five what? You still have x's.

---

##### The Invisible 1

A variable written alone has a coefficient of $1$, even though nobody writes it.

$$x \text{ means } 1x \qquad -y \text{ means } -1y$$

This trips up more students than it should. In $7n - n$, that second term is not nothing and it is not zero. It is $1n$. So $7n - n = 6n$, not $7n$.

Whenever you see a lonely variable, say "one" in your head before you say the letter.

---

##### Sorting a Longer Expression

When an expression has more than one family of terms, do not try to hold it all in your head. Sort first, then combine.

**Example 2:** Simplify $6x + 4 - 2x + 9$.

Step 1: Mark each term with the sign in front of it.
- $+6x$, $+4$, $-2x$, $+9$

Step 2: Group the like terms. The x terms go with the x terms, the constants with the constants.
- x terms: $6x - 2x$
- constants: $4 + 9$

Step 3: Combine each group.
- $6x - 2x = 4x$
- $4 + 9 = 13$

Step 4: Write the result, variable term first.
- $4x + 13$

That is the final answer. It cannot go further, because $4x$ and $13$ are not like terms. A student who writes $17x$ here has combined a variable term with a constant, which is the quarters-and-dimes mistake.

Notice Step 1. Carrying the sign with its term is the single habit that prevents most of the errors on this topic. The $-2x$ moves as a unit. The minus is part of the term.

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

Now the big one. Read this section twice.

**A minus sign in front of parentheses applies to everything inside, not just the first thing.**

**Example 3:** Simplify $4x - (x - 3)$.

Here is the wrong path, and it is wrong in a way that feels completely reasonable:

> "Drop the parentheses and keep going: $4x - x - 3 = 3x - 3$."

That is the most common wrong answer on this entire topic. The student handled the $x$ correctly and then let the $-3$ walk through the parentheses untouched.

Here is the right path. Subtracting a group means subtracting **every** member of the group. Rewrite the minus sign as $-1$ and hand it to each term inside:

- $-1 \times x = -x$
- $-1 \times (-3) = +3$

So:

$$4x - (x - 3) = 4x - x + 3 = 3x + 3$$

The $-3$ became $+3$. Two negatives make a positive, and this is exactly where that rule earns its keep.

**Prove it to yourself.** Pick a number for $x$ and test both answers against the original. Let $x = 2$.

- Original: $4(2) - (2 - 3) = 8 - (-1) = 8 + 1 = 9$
- Correct answer: $3(2) + 3 = 6 + 3 = 9$. Match.
- Wrong answer: $3(2) - 3 = 6 - 3 = 3$. Not a match.

That substitution check takes fifteen seconds and it will catch this error every single time. When an answer choice differs from another only by a sign, plug in a number.

---

##### Distributing, Then Combining

When a number sits outside parentheses, multiply it by every term inside. That is the **distributive property**. Then, and only then, combine.

**Example 4:** Simplify $2(3x + 4) - 5x$.

Step 1: Distribute the $2$ to **both** terms inside.
- $2 \times 3x = 6x$
- $2 \times 4 = 8$
- The expression is now $6x + 8 - 5x$

Step 2: Group and combine.
- x terms: $6x - 5x = 1x$, which we write as $x$
- constants: just the $8$

Step 3: Write the result.
- $x + 8$

Step 4: Check by substitution. Let $x = 2$.
- Original: $2(3 \times 2 + 4) - 5(2) = 2(10) - 10 = 10$
- Answer: $2 + 8 = 10$. Match.

The trap in Step 1 is stopping halfway. A student who multiplies $2 \times 3x$ and then forgets the $4$ gets $6x + 4 - 5x = x + 4$, which is wrong by exactly the amount that was skipped. The number outside touches **every** term inside. Draw two arrows if it helps.

---

##### When the Outside Number Is Negative

Combine the last two ideas and you have the hardest version of this problem.

**Example 5:** Simplify $6x - 3(2x - 1)$.

The $-3$ is a single package: a negative three, multiplied into both terms.

- $-3 \times 2x = -6x$
- $-3 \times (-1) = +3$

So the expression becomes $6x - 6x + 3$.

- x terms: $6x - 6x = 0$
- What remains: $3$

The answer is $3$. The x terms cancelled completely, which is allowed and which the test does use.

The two sign checkpoints here are the ones to slow down for: negative times positive is negative, and negative times negative is positive. Write the signs down before you write the numbers.

---

##### The Five Traps

Before you practice, know what is hunting you:

1. **Combining unlike terms.** $4x + 13$ does not become $17x$. Variables and constants never merge.
2. **Losing a negative through parentheses.** $4x - (x - 3)$ is $3x + 3$, never $3x - 3$. The minus reaches every term inside.
3. **Distributing to the first term only.** $2(3x + 4)$ is $6x + 8$, never $6x + 4$. The outside number touches everything.
4. **Adding when the sign says subtract.** In $8y - 3y$, the coefficient work is $8 - 3 = 5$, not $8 + 3 = 11$. Read the sign in front of each term.
5. **Ignoring the invisible 1.** In $7n - n$, that last term is $1n$. The answer is $6n$, not $7n$.

Every one of those is a habit, and habits can be retrained. When you miss a problem below, name the trap you fell into. Naming it is how you stop repeating it.

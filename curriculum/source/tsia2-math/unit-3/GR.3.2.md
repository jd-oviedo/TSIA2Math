#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Two Triangles Worth Memorising

GR.3.1 gave you the Pythagorean theorem, which works on every right triangle. It also takes three lines of arithmetic every time. Two shapes come up so often that their answers are worth knowing outright, and on a timed test that is the difference between finishing and not.

Both are still ordinary right triangles. Nothing here replaces the Pythagorean theorem. These are shortcuts to the same answer.

The figure shows a right triangle with both legs labelled $5$, both acute angles marked $45^\circ$, and the hypotenuse labelled $5\sqrt{2}$. Because the two acute angles are equal, the two legs are equal, and they are drawn the same length.

$$\text{leg} : \text{leg} : \text{hypotenuse} \;=\; 1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$$

Two rules, and they are opposites of each other:

- **Leg to hypotenuse: multiply by $\sqrt{2}$.**
- **Hypotenuse to leg: divide by $\sqrt{2}$.**

**Example 1:** Legs of $5$.

$$\text{hypotenuse} = 5\sqrt{2}$$

Check it with the Pythagorean theorem: $5^{2} + 5^{2} = 50$, and $\sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$. Note that $50$ is not the answer. It is the square of the answer, and stopping there is the single most common slip in this topic.

**Example 2:** Hypotenuse of $8\sqrt{2}$.

$$\text{leg} = \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = 8$$

Multiplying by $\sqrt{2}$ instead gives $16$, twice the truth. **The leg is always the shorter one**, so if your answer for a leg is bigger than the hypotenuse you handed in, you have gone the wrong way.

This is the triangle you get by cutting a square along its diagonal, which is why a square of side $s$ has a diagonal of $s\sqrt{2}$.

The figure shows a right triangle with a short leg of $6$ opposite the $30^\circ$ angle, a long leg of $6\sqrt{3}$ opposite the $60^\circ$ angle, and a hypotenuse of $12$. The long leg is drawn about $1.7$ times the short leg and the hypotenuse exactly twice it.

$$\text{short} : \text{long} : \text{hypotenuse} \;=\; 1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$$

**Everything is measured from the short leg.** Find it first, then build outward.

- **Short leg to hypotenuse: multiply by $2$.**
- **Short leg to long leg: multiply by $\sqrt{3}$.**
- **Coming the other way: divide.**

The short leg is opposite the $30^\circ$ angle. Always. The smallest angle faces the smallest side, and that is not a rule about special triangles, it is true of every triangle.

**Example 3:** Short leg $6$.

- Hypotenuse: $6 \times 2 = 12$
- Long leg: $6\sqrt{3}$, which is about $10.4$

Check the ordering: $6 < 10.4 < 12$. Short, long, hypotenuse, in that order, every time.

**Example 4:** Hypotenuse $14$.

$$\text{short} = \frac{14}{2} = 7, \qquad \text{long} = 7\sqrt{3}$$

Go to the short leg first, then out to whatever was asked. Trying to get from the hypotenuse straight to the long leg in one step is where most errors start.

**Example 5:** Long leg $9\sqrt{3}$.

$$\text{short} = \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = 9, \qquad \text{hypotenuse} = 18$$

---

##### Do Not Mix the Two Families

The $\sqrt{2}$ belongs to the $45$-$45$-$90$. The $2$ and the $\sqrt{3}$ belong to the $30$-$60$-$90$. Borrowing across is a whole error family of its own.

| Given | 45-45-90 | 30-60-90 |
|---|---|---|
| the factor that doubles | none | short to hypotenuse |
| the radical factor | $\sqrt{2}$, leg to hypotenuse | $\sqrt{3}$, short to long |
| equal sides | the two legs | none |

If a triangle has a $45^\circ$ angle in it, nothing in it doubles. If it has a $30^\circ$ angle in it, nothing in it uses $\sqrt{2}$.

---

##### Sanity Checks That Cost Nothing

1. **The hypotenuse is the longest side.** No leg may exceed it.
2. **$\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$ and $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.73$.** Turn your radical into a decimal and see whether the sides come out in a sensible order.
3. **The square is not the side.** $\sqrt{50}$ is about $7.1$, not $50$.

---

##### One More Shape Worth Knowing

An equilateral triangle cut down the middle is two $30$-$60$-$90$ triangles. For a side of $10$, the cut halves the base to $5$, which becomes the **short leg**, and the height is the long leg:

$$\text{height} = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.7$$

The full side of $10$ is the hypotenuse, not the short leg. Multiplying $10$ by $\sqrt{3}$ gives $17.3$, which is taller than the triangle is wide, and a glance at the shape rules that out.

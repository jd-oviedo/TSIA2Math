The figure shows a triangle with a base of $8$ and a dashed perpendicular height marked with a question mark. Its area is $24$ square units.

The triangle formula multiplies by **two** things: the base and the one-half. Both have to be undone.

$$\frac{1}{2}bh = 24 \quad \rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{2}(8)h = 24 \quad \rightarrow \quad 4h = 24 \quad \rightarrow \quad h = 6$$

Dividing only by the base gives $24 \div 8 = 3$, which forgets the one-half entirely and lands at half the true height. The reliable order is: **multiply by 2 first to clear the fraction, then divide by the base.**

$$h = \frac{2A}{b} = \frac{48}{8} = 6$$

Check forward: $\frac{1}{2}(8)(6) = 24$. Correct, and that forward check catches every version of this error.

Note the direction. Clearing a one-half means **multiplying** by 2. Dividing by 2 as well pushes you further from the answer, to $1.5$.

##### Undoing a Square

When the unknown is squared, dividing is not enough. You have to take the root as well.

**Example 1:** A square has area $49$. Find its side.

$$s^{2} = 49 \quad \rightarrow \quad s = \sqrt{49} = 7$$

**Example 2:** A circle has area $153.86$. Find its radius.

$$\pi r^{2} = 153.86 \quad \rightarrow \quad r^{2} = \frac{153.86}{3.14} = 49 \quad \rightarrow \quad r = 7$$

Two undoings, and the second is the one that gets skipped. Stopping at $49$ reports $r^{2}$, a squared length, where a length was asked for.

**If the formula squared something, your last step is a square root.**

---

##### Trapezoids: Recovering a Base

**Example 3:** A trapezoid has area $40$, height $5$, and one base of $6$. Find the other base.

$$\frac{1}{2}(6 + b)(5) = 40$$

Clear the one-half and the height:

$$6 + b = \frac{2 \times 40}{5} = 16$$

That $16$ is the **sum** of the two bases, not a base. Subtract the one you know:

$$b = 16 - 6 = 10$$

**Reporting the 16 is the standard error here.** Read the last line of the question again before you answer.

---

##### Perimeter Works the Same Way

**Example 4:** A rectangle has perimeter $30$ and width $5$. Find its length.

$$2(l + 5) = 30 \quad \rightarrow \quad l + 5 = 15 \quad \rightarrow \quad l = 10$$

Halving first is what makes this quick: $15$ is the semi-perimeter, one length plus one width. Reporting the $15$ answers a different question.

And keep perimeter and area apart. An area of $48$ and a perimeter of $48$ lead to completely different missing sides, so check which word the question used.

---

##### Two-Step Questions

Some items ask for something the missing dimension only leads to.

**Example 5:** A square has area $121$. Find its perimeter.

- Side: $\sqrt{121} = 11$
- Perimeter: $4 \times 11 = 44$

The $11$ is a stepping stone. **Name the unit of what the question asked for** and check that your answer has it.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Undoing only one factor.** A triangle's formula multiplies by the base and by one-half; undo both.
2. **Dividing where you should multiply.** Clearing a one-half means multiplying by 2.
3. **Stopping at the square.** Take the root when the formula squared something.
4. **Reporting a sum, or a stepping stone.** The sum of two bases is not a base, and a side is not a perimeter.

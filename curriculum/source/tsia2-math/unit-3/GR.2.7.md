# GR.2.7 - Expressing Measurement with Algebraic Expressions

**Topic ID:** GR.2.7

**Assessment Layer:** CRC

#### **Learning Objectives**

- Write and simplify perimeter, area, and volume expressions with a variable dimension, distributing a multiplied factor to every term in a binomial.
- Expand a squared binomial fully, including the middle term, rather than squaring each term separately.
- Verify an algebraic expression by substituting a specific number and checking it against the geometric quantity it should represent.

Two different expressions from the same rectangle, because they answer two different questions. Reporting $4x + 12$ when the perimeter was asked for is the same confusion as GR.2.3, wearing algebra as a disguise.

---

##### The Trap Everyone Falls Into

Distributing to the variable and stopping.

$$2(x + 3) = 2x + 3 \quad \color{#B23A2E}{\textbf{WRONG}}$$

$$2(x + 3) = 2x + 6 \quad \color{#2E7D5B}{\textbf{RIGHT}}$$

The $2$ multiplies **everything inside the parentheses**, not just the part with the letter on it. Test it with a number: if $x = 5$, the side is $8$, and doubling it gives $16$. The correct expression gives $2(5) + 6 = 16$. The wrong one gives $2(5) + 3 = 13$.

**Substituting a number to check is the whole defence.** Any time you are unsure whether an expression is right, pick a value for $x$, work the geometry with real numbers, and see whether your expression agrees.

The figure shows a square with both its base and its right side labelled $x + 2$, with right angles marked at all four corners. It is drawn in the proportions it would have if $x$ were $4$, so each side is six.

$$A = (x + 2)^{2} = (x + 2)(x + 2) = x^{2} + 2x + 2x + 4 = x^{2} + 4x + 4$$

Squaring a binomial is **not** squaring each piece.

$$(x + 2)^{2} = x^{2} + 4 \quad \color{#B23A2E}{\textbf{WRONG}}$$

The middle term is real. Check it with $x = 4$: the square has side $6$ and area $36$. The correct expression gives $16 + 16 + 4 = 36$. The wrong one gives $16 + 4 = 20$, and $20$ is not the area of anything here.

---

##### Where the Middle Term Comes From

Cut the side $x + 2$ square into four pieces at the point that splits each side into $x$ and $2$:

| Piece | Dimensions | Area |
|---|---|---|
| large corner | $x$ by $x$ | $x^{2}$ |
| strip | $x$ by $2$ | $2x$ |
| strip | $2$ by $x$ | $2x$ |
| small corner | $2$ by $2$ | $4$ |

Two strips, so $4x$. That is the middle term, and it is missing from $x^{2} + 4$ because that answer forgets the strips exist.

---

##### Curves and Solids Behave the Same Way

**Example 1:** A circle of radius $x + 1$.

$$A = \pi(x + 1)^{2} = \pi(x^{2} + 2x + 1)$$

$$C = 2\pi(x + 1) = 2\pi x + 2\pi$$

Squared for the area, not for the circumference. Same distinction as GR.2.2, same trap.

**Example 2:** A cone of radius $x$ and height $6$.

$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi x^{2}(6) = 2\pi x^{2}$$

The one third from GR.2.6 does not go away because a letter appeared. Answering $6\pi x^{2}$ drops it.

**Example 3:** A triangle of base $2x$ and height $6$.

$$A = \frac{1}{2}(2x)(6) = 6x$$

Halve, then multiply by the height. Halving the base to $x$ and stopping gives $x$, which forgets the height entirely.

---

##### Fractions Apply to the Whole Bracket

**Example 4:** A trapezoid with bases $x$ and $x + 6$ and height $4$.

$$A = \frac{1}{2}\big(x + (x + 6)\big)(4) = \frac{1}{2}(2x + 6)(4) = (x + 3)(4) = 4x + 12$$

The one half halves **both** terms: $2x$ becomes $x$ and $6$ becomes $3$. Halving only the $2x$ leaves $(x + 6)(4) = 4x + 24$, which is too big by $12$ no matter what $x$ is.

Check with $x = 2$: the bases are $2$ and $8$, the height is $4$, and the area is $\frac{1}{2}(10)(4) = 20$. The correct expression gives $4(2) + 12 = 20$. The other gives $32$.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Distributing to the variable but not the constant.** $2(x + 3)$ is $2x + 6$.
2. **Squaring a binomial term by term.** The middle term is two strips of real area.
3. **Applying a fraction to one term only, or to the base and not the height.**
4. **Inventing a term.** Every term in your answer has to come from a dimension you were given.

And the defence for all four: **substitute a number and check.**

Step 3: Check with $x = 3$ and $\pi \approx 3.14$. The cone is $\frac{1}{3}(3.14)(9)(6) = 56.52$, and $2(3.14)(9) = 56.52$.

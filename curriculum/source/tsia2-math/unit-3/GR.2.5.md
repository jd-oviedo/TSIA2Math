#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Wrapping Paper, Not Filling

Surface area is how much material it takes to **cover** a solid. Think wrapping paper, or the paint on the outside. It is measured in square units, exactly like the flat areas in GR.2.3, because it is a sum of flat areas.

The method never changes: **list every face, find each one's area, add them.** No solid needs a formula you have not already met.

The figure shows a rectangular prism with a length of $5$, a height of $4$, and a depth of $3$. The hidden back edges are dashed.

It has six faces in three matching pairs:

- front and back: $5 \times 4 = 20$ each
- left and right: $4 \times 3 = 12$ each
- top and bottom: $5 \times 3 = 15$ each

$$SA = 2(20) + 2(12) + 2(15) = 40 + 24 + 30 = 94$$

Or in one line, $SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)$.

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

You find the three distinct faces and forget that each has a twin.

Computing $20 + 12 + 15 = 47$ counts one of each pair and covers exactly half the solid. The answer is always precisely double what it should be, which makes it hard to spot by feel.

**Count the faces before you add.** A box has six. If your working shows three multiplications and no doubling, you are covering half a box.

Its cousin is confusing surface area with volume. For this prism, $5 \times 4 \times 3 = 60$ is the **volume**, in cubic units, and it is not a covering at all. **Surface area sums flat pieces; volume multiplies three lengths.**

The figure shows a cylinder with a radius of $3$, marked by a dashed line across the top face, and a height of $10$, dimensioned on a separate line beside the body.

Unroll it and there are three pieces: two circular ends and one rectangle.

- Two circles: $2\pi r^{2} = 2(3.14)(9) = 56.52$
- The curved side, unrolled, is a rectangle whose width is the circumference: $2\pi r h = 2(3.14)(3)(10) = 188.4$

$$SA = 2\pi r^{2} + 2\pi r h = 56.52 + 188.4 = 244.92$$

The curved part alone is the **lateral surface**. Reporting only it, $188.4$, is the standard error, and it is what "lateral surface area" genuinely means when a question asks for it, so read which one is wanted.

##### How Many Bases?

This is where solids differ, and where the marks are.

| Solid | Bases | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| cylinder | two | $2\pi r^{2} + 2\pi r h$ |
| cone | one | $\pi r^{2} + \pi r l$ |
| square pyramid | one | $b^{2} + 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} b s$ |
| sphere | none | $4\pi r^{2}$ |

A cone and a pyramid come to a point, so they have **one** base. Giving them two, or leaving the one out, are the two errors this table exists to prevent.

**Example 1:** A cone with radius $3$ and slant height $5$.

$$\pi r^{2} + \pi r l = 3.14(9) + 3.14(3)(5) = 28.26 + 47.1 = 75.36$$

Note that the cone's formula uses the **slant height** $l$, the distance up the sloping side, not the perpendicular height. That is the one place in this unit where a slanted length is the right one to use.

**Example 2:** A square pyramid with base edge $6$ and slant height $5$.

- Base: $6^{2} = 36$
- Four triangular faces: $4 \times \frac{1}{2}(6)(5) = 60$
- Total: $96$

---

##### Open Solids and Joined Solids

Questions often remove a face or hide one.

**Example 3:** A cylindrical tub with **no lid**, radius $5$ and height $8$.

- One base only: $3.14(25) = 78.5$
- Lateral: $2(3.14)(5)(8) = 251.2$
- Total: $329.7$

**Example 4:** Two cubes of edge $3$ glued face to face.

Each cube alone has surface area $6 \times 9 = 54$, so the two together would be $108$. But the glued faces are on the **inside** now and no longer need covering, and there are **two** of them, one from each cube.

$$108 - 2(9) = 90$$

**When two solids are joined, remove both copies of the shared face.** Removing one is the near miss, at $99$.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Not doubling opposite faces.** A box has six faces in three pairs.
2. **Reporting the lateral surface only.** The bases count unless the question says open.
3. **Giving a cone or pyramid two bases.** They come to a point, so they have one.
4. **Leaving the shared face in a joined solid.** Remove both copies of it.

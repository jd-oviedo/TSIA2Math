#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Two Questions, Asked in Order

Every item in this topic offers four statements about one data set and asks which the data **supports**. Two questions decide it:

1. **What exactly was measured, and on whom?** The subjects, the variables, and the range of values actually observed.
2. **Does the statement stay inside all three?** A supported conclusion adds nothing the data did not measure.

**The confusion this topic exists to prevent is treating a pattern as an explanation.** Data can show that two things move together. It cannot, on its own, say which one moves the other, or whether a third thing moves both.

---

##### Association Is Not Causation

**Example 1:** A store finds that on warmer days, ice cream sales are higher. What does that support?

Step 1: What was measured? Daily temperature and daily sales, at this store.

Step 2: What is the pattern? They rose together.

Step 3: What does it support?
- On the days recorded, warmer weather went with higher sales. That is it.

Three different claims go beyond it, and each is its own error:

- **Warm weather causes the sales.** Possible, and not shown.
- **The sales cause the warm weather.** The same evidence, read backwards. Association has no direction in it.
- **Nothing else could explain both.** School holidays fall in warm months and raise sales on their own. A third variable that moves both is a **confounder**, and ruling one out takes evidence the data does not contain.

---

##### A Careful Study Is Still an Observational Study

Size and care do not turn observation into causation. A study of a million people that only *watched* still only shows what moved together.

What does support a causal claim is **assignment**: subjects put into groups by the researcher, at random. Then the groups differ only by the treatment, so a difference in outcome has one explanation left.

**"The study was large and well designed" is a statement about the study. "The treatment caused the outcome" is a statement about the world.** Only random assignment carries you from one to the other.

---

##### Two Ways to Over-Reach, and They Point Opposite

Here is the pair that costs the most marks, because the two errors look nothing alike and are the same mistake about strength.

**Example 2:** A scatterplot of $50$ students shows a moderate positive association between hours of sleep and test score.

- **Too strong:** "Students who sleep more **always** score higher." The data shows a tendency. A tendency has exceptions; "always" says it has none.
- **Too weak:** "There is **no** relationship, because the points do not lie on a line." A moderate association is a real finding. Demanding a perfect line throws away a result the data does support.

**Supported:** among these $50$ students, more sleep tended to go with higher scores.

The test for which error you are looking at: **does the statement claim more certainty than the data gives, or less?** Both are wrong, and they are wrong in opposite directions.

---

##### Two Ways a Model Stops Applying, and They Are Not the Same

**Example 3:** A model fitted to students who studied $1$ to $6$ hours predicts $\text{score} = 10h + 40$.

- **Outside the range:** predicting the score for $20$ hours. The model never saw $20$ hours. Nothing about the fit says the pattern continues out there.
- **Outside the group:** predicting a score for a student at a different school, at $4$ hours. That is inside the range and outside the population.

**The test: did the statement change the $x$ value beyond what was observed, or change who is being described?** The first is extrapolation. The second is assuming the fit transfers. A statement can only be judged once you know which one it did.

---

##### A Sample Describes Its Own Group

Forty shoppers at one store describe those forty shoppers. Extending them to all shoppers is a separate claim, and it needs the sample to be representative of that wider group.

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

**Choosing the statement that sounds most like a finding.** Causal language reads like a result, and the supported answer usually reads like a restatement. It feels like it is not saying enough.

That feeling is the trap. A supported conclusion **is** close to a restatement, because everything past a restatement is something the data did not measure. Read each choice and ask what it claims that was never observed. The one that claims nothing extra is the answer.

---

##### The Five Traps

1. **Association read as causation.** Moving together is not moving each other.
2. **Causation read backwards.** The same data fits both directions equally.
3. **A confounder ignored.** A third variable can move both, and ruling it out needs evidence.
4. **A tendency stated as a rule.** "Always" claims what "tends to" does not.
5. **A model used outside its range or its group.** Different failures, different tests.

#### **Part 2: Practice Problems**

Solve each problem. Show your thinking.

**Basic Level** (try these first)

1. A school survey found that students who ate breakfast reported feeling more alert in first period than students who skipped it. Which conclusion is directly supported?
   - A) Among the students surveyed, those who ate breakfast reported more alertness than those who did not
   - B) Eating breakfast causes students to feel more alert
   - C) Feeling alert causes students to eat breakfast
   - D) Nothing can be concluded, because a survey cannot show anything

2. A store found that on warmer days, its ice cream sales were higher. Which conclusion is directly supported?
   - A) Warmer weather causes people to buy more ice cream
   - B) On the days recorded, warmer weather went with higher sales
   - C) Higher ice cream sales cause the weather to get warmer
   - D) Warmer weather causes higher sales, and no other factor could be involved

3. A class of $30$ students took a test and the mean score was $85$. Which conclusion is supported?
   - A) Every student scored $85$
   - B) These $30$ scores predict what next year's class will score
   - C) The $30$ scores balance at $85$
   - D) Nothing can be concluded, because a mean says nothing about individuals

4. A weather log shows that it rained on $8$ of the past $10$ days in a town. Which conclusion is directly supported?
   - A) It rains about $80\%$ of the time in this town
   - B) It will rain in this town tomorrow
   - C) Nothing can be concluded, because $10$ days is too short a record
   - D) It rained on $8$ of those $10$ days

**Proficient Level** (these require an extra step)

5. A model fitted to students who studied between $1$ and $6$ hours predicts $\text{score} = 10h + 40$. Which statement is supported?
   - A) Within $1$ to $6$ hours, each extra hour of study went with about $10$ more points
   - B) A student who studied $20$ hours would score $240$
   - C) Studying causes higher scores
   - D) The same model gives the score for a student at a different school who studied $4$ hours

6. An observational study found that adults who exercise more have lower cholesterol. Which conclusion is supported?
   - A) Exercise lowers cholesterol
   - B) In this study, more exercise was associated with lower cholesterol
   - C) Because the study was large and carefully designed, it establishes that exercise lowers cholesterol
   - D) Lower cholesterol causes people to exercise more

7. A scatterplot of $50$ students shows a moderate positive association between hours of sleep and test score. Which conclusion is supported?
   - A) Students who sleep more always score higher
   - B) Sleeping more causes higher test scores
   - C) Among these $50$ students, more sleep tended to go with higher scores
   - D) There is no relationship, because the points do not lie on a line

**Advanced Level** (these need multiple steps or reverse thinking)

8. In a randomized experiment, students were assigned at random to receive tutoring or not, and the tutored group scored higher. Which conclusion is supported?
   - A) Nothing causal, because no study can ever show causation
   - B) Higher scores caused those students to be given tutoring
   - C) The result applies to all students everywhere
   - D) In this experiment, tutoring caused the higher scores

9. A town found that neighbourhoods with more libraries have higher reading scores. Which conclusion is supported?
   - A) Building libraries raises reading scores
   - B) Neighbourhoods with more libraries had higher scores, and differences such as household income were not measured
   - C) Because the data covers every neighbourhood in the town, the causal link is established
   - D) Libraries raise reading scores, and no other neighbourhood difference could explain it

10. A model fitted to daily temperatures from $10$ to $30$ degrees predicts one cafe's sales as $\text{sales} = 5T + 20$. Which use of the model is appropriate?
    - A) Predicting this cafe's sales at $40$ degrees
    - B) Predicting a different cafe's sales at $20$ degrees
    - C) Predicting this cafe's sales at $20$ degrees
    - D) Rejecting the model, because it cannot cover every temperature

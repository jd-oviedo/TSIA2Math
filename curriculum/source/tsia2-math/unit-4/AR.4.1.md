# AR.4.1 - Simplifying Polynomial Expressions

#### **Learning Objectives**

- Combine like terms by adding coefficients while leaving the variable and its exponent unchanged.
- Distribute a subtracted quantity across every term inside the parentheses, correctly flipping each sign.
- Distribute a coefficient into every term of its parentheses before combining like terms across multiple groups.

---

#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Sorting Before Counting

Empty a drawer of loose change onto a table and you sort before you count. Quarters with quarters, dimes with dimes. Nobody counts a pile of mixed coins by adding the objects together, because "seven coins" does not tell you how much money you have.

Simplifying a polynomial is that sort, then that count.

$$3x^{2} + 5x - x^{2} + 2x = 2x^{2} + 7x$$

The $x^{2}$ terms combined with each other. The $x$ terms combined with each other. Neither pile could merge with the other, and the result has two terms because there were two piles.

If you have already met combining like terms with plain $x$ terms, this is the same skill with one addition: **the exponent is now part of what has to match.**

---

##### What Makes Terms Alike

A **term** is one chunk of an expression, separated from its neighbors by a plus or minus sign.

**Like terms** have exactly the same variable **and** exactly the same exponent.

- $3x^{2}$ and $-7x^{2}$ are like terms. Same letter, same exponent.
- $5x$ and $2x$ are like terms.
- $3x^{2}$ and $5x$ are **not** like terms. Same letter, different exponent.
- $4ab$ and $-2ab$ are like terms. The whole variable part matches.
- $4ab$ and $3a$ are **not** like terms. One carries a $b$ and the other does not.

$x^{2}$ and $x$ are as different as quarters and dimes. $x^{2}$ means $x$ times $x$; $x$ means one $x$. Whatever $x$ turns out to be, those are different quantities, and no rule lets you merge them.

**The rule:** add or subtract the coefficients, and leave the variable part **completely untouched**.

$$3x^{2} + 5x^{2} = 8x^{2}$$

The coefficients went from $3$ and $5$ to $8$. The $x^{2}$ did not change at all. It did not become $x^{4}$, and it did not disappear.

---

##### The Exponent Does Not Move

Two errors show up constantly here, and both are about the exponent.

**Exponents do not add when you combine like terms.**

$$2x^{2} + 3x^{2} = 5x^{2} \quad \text{not} \quad 5x^{4}$$

Test it with $x = 2$. Then $2x^{2} = 8$ and $3x^{2} = 12$, so the sum is $20$. Check the candidates: $5x^{2} = 5 \times 4 = 20$. Match. And $5x^{4} = 5 \times 16 = 80$. Not a match.

Exponents add when you **multiply** terms, which is a different operation from combining them. Adding is counting how many you have.

**An unpaired term does not vanish.**

In $2x^{2} + 3x^{2} + x$, the $x$ has nothing to pair with. It stays exactly where it is:

$$2x^{2} + 3x^{2} + x = 5x^{2} + x$$

Answering $5x^{2}$ throws away a whole term because it was inconvenient. A leftover term is part of the answer.

---

##### Carry the Sign With the Term

Sort with signs attached. The minus in front of a term belongs to that term.

**Example 1:** Simplify $3x^{2} + 5x - x^{2} + 2x$.

Step 1: Label each term with the sign in front of it.
- $+3x^{2}$, $+5x$, $-x^{2}$, $+2x$

Step 2: Group by pile.
- $x^{2}$ terms: $+3x^{2}$ and $-x^{2}$
- $x$ terms: $+5x$ and $+2x$

Step 3: Combine each pile. Remember that $-x^{2}$ means $-1x^{2}$.
- $3 - 1 = 2$, so $2x^{2}$
- $5 + 2 = 7$, so $7x$

Step 4: Write the result, highest exponent first.
- $2x^{2} + 7x$

Step 3 has two traps in it. Reading $-x^{2}$ as $-0$ leaves $3x^{2}$, and reading its minus sign as applying to the $+2x$ as well turns $5 + 2$ into $5 - 2$. **The sign attaches to the one term it sits in front of, and a bare variable carries a coefficient of $1$.**

---

##### Constants Are Their Own Pile

**Example 2:** Simplify $5a + 7 + 3a + 4$.

Step 1: Sort.
- $a$ terms: $5a$, $3a$
- constants: $7$, $4$

Step 2: Combine each.
- $5 + 3 = 8$, so $8a$
- $7 + 4 = 11$

Step 3: Result.
- $8a + 11$

It cannot go further. $8a$ and $11$ are different piles.

Answering $19a$ adds every number in sight, which is the mixed-coins mistake. Answering $15a + 11$ multiplies the coefficients instead of adding them: combining is counting, and $5$ of something plus $3$ of something is $8$ of it, never $15$.

---

##### Three or More Piles

**Example 3:** Simplify $4x^{3} - 2x^{2} + x^{3} + 5x^{2} - 3x$.

Step 1: Sort into piles by exponent, keeping signs.
- $x^{3}$: $+4x^{3}$, $+x^{3}$
- $x^{2}$: $-2x^{2}$, $+5x^{2}$
- $x$: $-3x$

Step 2: Combine each pile.
- $4 + 1 = 5$, so $5x^{3}$
- $-2 + 5 = 3$, so $+3x^{2}$
- $-3x$ has no partner and stays as it is

Step 3: Write in order of decreasing exponent, which is called **standard form**.
- $5x^{3} + 3x^{2} - 3x$

Step 2's middle line is the one to slow down on. The pile is $-2$ and $+5$, and it sums to **positive** $3$. A student who writes $-3x^{2}$ has computed the size correctly and then kept the sign of the first term out of habit. **The sign of the answer comes from the arithmetic, not from whichever term you happened to see first.**

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

Read this section twice.

**A minus sign in front of parentheses applies to every term inside.**

**Example 4:** Simplify $8x^{2} + 2x - (3x^{2} - 4x)$.

Here is the wrong path, and it feels entirely reasonable:

> "Drop the parentheses: $8x^{2} + 2x - 3x^{2} - 4x = 5x^{2} - 2x$."

The $3x^{2}$ was handled correctly and the $-4x$ walked straight through the parentheses untouched. That is the single most common wrong answer on this topic.

The right path. Subtracting a group means subtracting **every member** of it. Treat the minus as $-1$ and hand it to each term inside:

- $-1 \times 3x^{2} = -3x^{2}$
- $-1 \times (-4x) = +4x$

So:

$$8x^{2} + 2x - 3x^{2} + 4x = 5x^{2} + 6x$$

The $-4x$ became $+4x$. Two negatives make a positive, and this is where that rule earns its keep.

**Prove it to yourself.** Let $x = 1$.
- Original: $8 + 2 - (3 - 4) = 10 - (-1) = 11$
- Correct answer: $5 + 6 = 11$. Match.
- Wrong answer: $5 - 2 = 3$. Not a match.

That check takes fifteen seconds and catches this error every time. **When two answer choices differ only by a sign, substitute a number.**

---

##### Distribute Into Everything, Then Combine

**Example 5:** Simplify $2(3x^{2} - x) + 4(x^{2} + 5)$.

Step 1: Distribute each outside number into **both** terms of its own bracket.
- $2 \times 3x^{2} = 6x^{2}$ and $2 \times (-x) = -2x$
- $4 \times x^{2} = 4x^{2}$ and $4 \times 5 = 20$

Step 2: Write out what you now have.
- $6x^{2} - 2x + 4x^{2} + 20$

Step 3: Sort and combine.
- $x^{2}$: $6 + 4 = 10$, so $10x^{2}$
- $x$: $-2x$ alone
- constant: $20$ alone

Step 4: Result.
- $10x^{2} - 2x + 20$

The trap in Step 1 is stopping halfway through a bracket. Multiplying $4 \times x^{2}$ and forgetting the $5$ leaves a $5$ where a $20$ belongs. **Each outside number touches every term inside its own parentheses.** Draw the arrows.

---

##### Distributing a Negative

**Example 6:** Which expression is equivalent to $3(2x - 4) - 2(x - 5)$?

Step 1: Distribute the $3$ into both terms.
- $6x - 12$

Step 2: Distribute the $-2$ into both terms. It is a single package: negative two.
- $-2 \times x = -2x$
- $-2 \times (-5) = +10$

Step 3: Assemble.
- $6x - 12 - 2x + 10$

Step 4: Sort and combine.
- $x$ terms: $6 - 2 = 4$, so $4x$
- constants: $-12 + 10 = -2$

Step 5: Result.
- $4x - 2$

Step 2 is the whole item. $-2 \times -5$ is $+10$, so the constants **partly cancel** and land on $-2$. A student who lets the $-5$ through unchanged gets $-12 - 10 = -22$.

Check with $x = 1$: original is $3(-2) - 2(-4) = -6 + 8 = 2$, and $4(1) - 2 = 2$. Match.

---

##### The Five Traps

1. **Merging different exponents.** $2x^{2}$ and $5x$ are different piles. $2x^{2} + 5x$ is already simplified.
2. **Adding exponents while combining.** $2x^{2} + 3x^{2}$ is $5x^{2}$, never $5x^{4}$.
3. **Losing a negative through parentheses.** $-(3x^{2} - 4x)$ is $-3x^{2} + 4x$. The minus reaches every term.
4. **Distributing to the first term only.** $4(x^{2} + 5)$ is $4x^{2} + 20$, never $4x^{2} + 5$.
5. **Keeping the first term's sign.** The pile $-2x^{2}$ and $+5x^{2}$ combines to $+3x^{2}$, not $-3x^{2}$.

Substituting a number into the original and into your answer catches every one of these. When you miss a problem below, name the trap. Naming it is how you stop repeating it.

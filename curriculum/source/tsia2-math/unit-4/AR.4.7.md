#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Substitute Into Both Halves

A rational function is a fraction with a variable in it. Evaluating one means putting a number in for the variable and working out what comes out.

$$f(x) = \frac{x + 5}{x - 1}$$

To find $f(3)$, replace **every** $x$ with $3$. Every one, top and bottom.

$$f(3) = \frac{3 + 5}{3 - 1} = \frac{8}{2} = 4$$

That is the whole procedure. The difficulty is not the arithmetic; it is remembering that the denominator is part of the problem.

**Every $x$ in the expression becomes the number.** The bar does not protect the bottom half from the substitution.

##### Work Top and Bottom Separately, Then Divide

The fraction bar groups. It acts like brackets around the numerator and brackets around the denominator, even though nobody writes them.

$$f(x) = \frac{2x + 4}{x - 3}, \qquad f(5) = ?$$

Step 1: Numerator. $2(5) + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14$.

Step 2: Denominator. $5 - 3 = 2$.

Step 3: Divide, last.

$$\frac{14}{2} = 7$$

**Finish each half completely before dividing.** Dividing partway through gives the wrong answer, because a fraction bar is not a series of separate little divisions.

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

**Substituting into the numerator and forgetting the denominator.**

$$f(x) = \frac{x + 7}{x + 1}, \qquad f(2)$$

The wrong version substitutes into the top, gets $9$, and reports $9$.

The denominator was never evaluated at all. The answer should be:

$$f(2) = \frac{2 + 7}{2 + 1} = \frac{9}{3} = 3$$

**Why it happens:** the numerator is where your eye starts, and once it has produced a clean number the work feels finished. Nothing on the page objects.

**The fix is to write the fraction bar first, before either half.** Draw the bar, then fill in the top, then fill in the bottom. An empty denominator staring at you is impossible to walk past:

$$f(2) = \frac{2 + 7}{\;\;\;\;} \;\to\; \frac{9}{2 + 1} \;\to\; \frac{9}{3} = 3$$

**A related version of the same error** is reporting the numerator as the answer once you have both halves. If the question asks for $f(2)$ and you have written $\frac{9}{3}$, the answer is $3$. The $9$ is a step, not a result.

##### Read the Denominator Exactly

$$f(x) = \frac{10}{2x - 3}, \qquad f(4)$$

Step 1: Numerator. There is no $x$ on top, so it stays $10$.

Step 2: Denominator. $2(4) - 3 = 8 - 3 = 5$.

$$\frac{10}{5} = 2$$

**Misreading the denominator is easy and expensive.** Treating $2x - 3$ as $2(x - 3)$ gives $2(1) = 2$ and an answer of $5$ instead of $2$. The expression says multiply by two, then subtract three. It does not say subtract three, then double.

**Subtract in the order written.** $x - 3$ at $x = 4$ is $1$, not $-1$. Reversing it flips the sign of the denominator and therefore of the answer.

##### Dividing, Not Multiplying

$$f(x) = \frac{12}{x}, \qquad f(3)$$

$$f(3) = \frac{12}{3} = 4$$

Not $36$. The bar means divide.

This sounds too obvious to state, and it is still one of the standard errors, because $\frac{12}{x}$ and $12x$ look similar at a glance and behave in opposite directions. As $x$ grows, $12x$ grows and $\frac{12}{x}$ shrinks.

**A sanity check:** put in a bigger number and see which way the answer moves. $f(6) = \frac{12}{6} = 2$, which is smaller than $f(3) = 4$. If your answers grow as $x$ grows, you have multiplied.

##### Reduce the Final Fraction

The answer is not finished until the fraction is in lowest terms.

$$f(x) = \frac{x + 2}{x + 8}, \qquad f(4) = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$\frac{6}{12}$ is correct arithmetic but not a finished answer. Divide top and bottom by their common factor.

Some answers do not divide evenly, and that is fine. $\frac{7}{3}$ is a perfectly good final answer; it just cannot be reduced.

##### Negative Inputs

$$f(x) = \frac{x^{2}}{x + 5}, \qquad f(-2)$$

Step 1: Numerator. $(-2)^{2} = 4$. Squaring a negative gives a positive.

Step 2: Denominator. $-2 + 5 = 3$.

$$\frac{4}{3}$$

Brackets around the substituted value are worth writing every time. $-2^{2}$ read without brackets is $-4$, which is a different and wrong number.

##### The Five Traps

1. **Substituting into the numerator only.** Draw the fraction bar first so the empty denominator is impossible to ignore.
2. **Reporting the numerator as the answer.** $\frac{9}{3}$ means the answer is $3$.
3. **Misreading the denominator.** $2x - 3$ is not $2(x - 3)$. Follow the expression exactly as written.
4. **Subtracting in the wrong order.** $x - 3$ at $x = 4$ is $1$, not $-1$.
5. **Multiplying instead of dividing.** $\frac{12}{x}$ at $x = 3$ is $4$, not $36$. Check which way the answer moves as the input grows.

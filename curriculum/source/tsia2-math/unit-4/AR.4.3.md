#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Only Matching Terms Combine

You already do this with units. Three metres plus four metres is seven metres. Three metres plus four seconds is not seven of anything, because metres and seconds are not the same kind of thing.

Powers of $x$ behave exactly the same way.

$$3x^{2} + 4x^{2} = 7x^{2}$$

$$3x^{2} + 4x \quad\text{stays}\quad 3x^{2} + 4x$$

Terms combine only when they have the **same variable to the same power**. $x^{2}$ and $x$ are different kinds of thing, and no amount of wanting a shorter answer will merge them.

**Like terms means the exponents match.** The coefficients can be anything at all.

##### Adding: Group, Then Combine

$$(4x^{2} + 3x - 2) + (x^{2} - 5x + 7)$$

Step 1: Group by degree. Take the $x^{2}$ terms together, then the $x$ terms, then the constants.

- $x^{2}$: $4x^{2} + x^{2}$
- $x$: $3x - 5x$
- constants: $-2 + 7$

Step 2: Combine each group.

- $4 + 1 = 5$, giving $5x^{2}$
- $3 - 5 = -2$, giving $-2x$
- $-2 + 7 = 5$

$$5x^{2} - 2x + 5$$

**Every term has to end up somewhere.** If a term appeared in either polynomial, it appears in your grouping. Losing one is the quietest error in this topic, because the answer still looks like a polynomial.

##### Subtracting: The Minus Reaches Every Term

This is where the topic gets hard, and it is worth slowing down.

$$(7x + 3) - (2x + 8)$$

The minus sign in front of the bracket applies to **everything inside it**, not just the first term.

Step 1: Distribute the minus.

$$7x + 3 - 2x - 8$$

Notice the $+8$ became $-8$. That is the whole difficulty of this topic in one sign.

Step 2: Combine.

$$5x - 5$$

**Check by substituting.** At $x = 1$: the original is $(10) - (10) = 0$, and $5(1) - 5 = 0$. Agreement.

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

**Applying the subtraction to only the first term inside the bracket.**

$$(7x + 3) - (2x + 8) \quad\text{written as}\quad 7x + 3 - 2x + 8$$

The $2x$ got its sign changed. The $8$ did not. The answer comes out $5x + 11$ instead of $5x - 5$, and the error is invisible in the final expression, which looks like a perfectly ordinary polynomial.

**Why it happens:** the minus is written once and has to be remembered several times. Nothing on the page reminds you.

**The fix is to rewrite before you combine.** Do not try to distribute in your head while also collecting like terms. Write the second polynomial out with every sign already flipped, on its own line, and only then combine:

$$(7x + 3) - (2x + 8) \;\to\; 7x + 3 \;\underbrace{-\,2x - 8}_{\text{every sign flipped}}$$

One extra line. It removes the error entirely, because by the time you are combining, there is no minus left to forget.

**And check with a number.** At $x = 1$, the wrong answer $5x + 11$ gives $16$, while the original gives $0$. A substitution catches this every time.

##### Subtracting a Negative

$$(3x^{2} - 5x + 1) - (-2x^{2} + 4x - 6)$$

The rule has not changed: flip every sign in the second bracket.

- $-2x^{2}$ becomes $+2x^{2}$
- $+4x$ becomes $-4x$
- $-6$ becomes $+6$

$$3x^{2} - 5x + 1 + 2x^{2} - 4x + 6 = 5x^{2} - 9x + 7$$

**Subtracting a negative adds.** Flipping the sign of $-2x^{2}$ gives $+2x^{2}$, so the $x^{2}$ terms grow rather than shrink. Students who read that first term as still negative get $x^{2}$ instead of $5x^{2}$, and the rest of the answer is fine, which makes it easy to miss.

##### When a Term Vanishes

$$(4x^{3} - x^{2} + 6) + (2x^{3} + x^{2} - 6)$$

Group and combine:

- $x^{3}$: $4 + 2 = 6$, giving $6x^{3}$
- $x^{2}$: $-1 + 1 = 0$
- constants: $6 - 6 = 0$

$$6x^{3}$$

A one-term answer is not a sign that you dropped something. Here the $x^{2}$ terms and the constants genuinely cancelled. **Cancelling to zero and forgetting to write a term look identical in the answer**, which is why the grouping step matters: if you wrote every term into a group, you know the zeros were earned.

##### Degree Does Not Change

Adding and subtracting never creates a new power. If the highest power going in was $x^{3}$, the highest power coming out is $x^{3}$ or lower, never $x^{4}$ or $x^{5}$.

That is a fast sanity check. An answer with a power that appeared in neither original polynomial is wrong, and usually the cause is combining terms of different degree into one.

##### The Five Traps

1. **Distributing the minus to only the first term.** $-(2x + 8)$ is $-2x - 8$. Rewrite the whole bracket with flipped signs before combining.
2. **Combining terms of different degree.** $3x^{2} + 4x$ does not become $7x^{3}$ or $7x$. Different powers stay apart.
3. **Losing a term.** Every term from both polynomials lands in a group. If your answer has fewer terms than expected, check whether they cancelled or whether one was never carried.
4. **Reading a subtracted negative as still negative.** $-(-2x^{2})$ is $+2x^{2}$. Subtracting a negative makes it bigger.
5. **Flipping a constant's sign for no reason.** In addition, nothing changes sign. The flipping only happens when a bracket is subtracted.

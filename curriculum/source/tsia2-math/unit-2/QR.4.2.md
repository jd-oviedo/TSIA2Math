#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### A Proportional Part and a Fixed Part

Almost every problem in this topic has the same shape:

$$\text{total} = (\text{rate} \times \text{amount}) + \text{fixed charge}$$

The rate part is proportional: double the amount and it doubles. The fixed part is not: it is there once whatever you do. Mixing them up is the whole difficulty, and it was already the difficulty in QR.3.1 and QR.4.1. What is new here is that the **amount** often has to be computed first, from a proportion.

A taxi charges \$3.50 to start plus \$2 a mile. Eight miles costs

$$2(8) + 3.50 = 19.50$$

**Do the proportional part, then add the fixed part. Never merge them.** Merging gives $5.50 \times 8 = 44$, which charges the flag-drop fee eight times.

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

Running the problem backward, you divide before removing the fee.

A gym bill was \$205, made up of a \$25 joining fee and \$30 a month. How many months?

The instinct is $205 \div 30 = 6.83$ months. That is wrong because \$25 of that \$205 never had anything to do with months. It has to come out first.

$$205 - 25 = 180, \qquad 180 \div 30 = 6$$

**Peel the fixed charge off the total before you divide.** Forward you multiply then add; backward you subtract then divide, in that order, because you are undoing the steps in reverse.

The check is fast: run your answer forward. $30(6) + 25 = 205$. Correct. Running 6.83 forward gives $30(6.83) + 25 = 229.90$, not \$205, so it was never right.

---

##### Getting the Amount From a Proportion First

**Example 1:** A car goes 32 miles on a gallon. Gas costs \$3.40 a gallon. A 480-mile trip also incurs \$25 in tolls. What is the total cost?

Three steps, in order, and each one has its own units.

- Gallons needed: $480 \div 32 = 15$ gallons
- Fuel cost: $15 \times 3.40 = 51$ dollars
- Total: $51 + 25 = 76$ dollars

Every intermediate value here is a real quantity and none of them is the answer. 15 is gallons, 51 is dollars before tolls, 76 is the answer.

The dangerous shortcut is multiplying the miles by the price directly: $480 \times 3.40 = 1632$. That treats miles as gallons, and it is off by the fuel economy factor. **Check that the units of what you are multiplying actually cancel.** Gallons times dollars-per-gallon gives dollars. Miles times dollars-per-gallon gives nothing.

---

##### Counting the Periods

**Example 2:** A subscription costs \$12 a month plus a \$20 setup fee. What does two years cost?

The rate is per **month** and the question is in **years**, so convert before you multiply.

- $2$ years $= 24$ months
- $12(24) + 20 = 288 + 20 = 308$

Answering $12(2) + 20 = 44$ uses two periods where there are twenty-four. **Make the period in your multiplication match the period in the rate.** This is the QR.2.6 conversion habit showing up inside a linear model.

---

##### Layering a Percentage On Top

**Example 3:** A caterer charges \$22 a guest plus a \$150 venue fee, and then adds $10\%$ tax to the whole bill. What do 40 guests cost?

Build the bill first, then tax all of it.

- Guests: $22(40) = 880$
- Add the venue fee: $880 + 150 = 1030$
- Tax the total: $1030 \times 1.10 = 1133$

Taxing before adding the fee gives $880(1.10) + 150 = 1118$, which leaves the venue fee untaxed. Whether that is right depends entirely on the wording, and here "to the whole bill" settles it.

**Bracket what the percentage applies to before you multiply**, exactly as in QR.3.3.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Merging the fee into the rate.** \$3.50 plus \$2 a mile is not \$5.50 a mile. Test at two distances.
2. **Dividing before subtracting the fee.** Backward, subtract then divide. Check by running your answer forward.
3. **Using the wrong number of periods.** Two years is 24 months when the rate is monthly.
4. **Multiplying quantities whose units do not cancel.** Miles times dollars per gallon is not a cost.

#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Two Rules, Both About Slope

Everything in this topic is decided by comparing slopes. Nothing else about the equations matters.

| Relationship | Slopes |
|---|---|
| **parallel** | equal: $m_2 = m_1$ |
| **perpendicular** | negative reciprocals: $m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$ |

Parallel is the easy one. A line parallel to $y = 4x - 3$ has slope 4, full stop. The intercept can be anything, and changing it slides the line without tilting it.

Perpendicular is where the errors live, because the rule has **two** parts and students routinely do one.

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

You do half of "negative reciprocal."

For a line perpendicular to $y = 2x + 5$, the slope is $-\frac{1}{2}$. Two operations happened: the 2 was flipped to $\frac{1}{2}$, **and** the sign was changed.

- Doing only the negation gives $-2$. Same steepness, wrong tilt: that line runs down as steeply as the original runs up, and the two are not perpendicular.
- Doing only the reciprocal gives $\frac{1}{2}$. Both climb, so they cannot cross at a right angle.

**Say both words as you do it.** Negative. Reciprocal. Flip the fraction and change the sign, and check that the two slopes have opposite signs when you finish.

There is a test that catches every version at once: **perpendicular slopes multiply to $-1$.**

$$2 \times -\frac{1}{2} = -1 \qquad \text{correct}$$
$$2 \times -2 = -4 \qquad \text{not perpendicular}$$
$$2 \times \frac{1}{2} = 1 \qquad \text{not perpendicular}$$

One multiplication, and it needs no memory of which operation you were supposed to do.

---

##### Parallel Is Not the Same as Identical

Two lines with the same slope come in two flavours, and questions do distinguish them.

- $y = 3x + 1$ and $y = 3x - 4$ share a slope and have **different** intercepts. They are parallel, and they never meet.
- $y = 3x + 1$ and $y = 3x + 1$ are the **same line**. Every point is shared.

Same line is not usually what "parallel" is meant to describe, so **check the intercepts too** before answering. If both the slope and the intercept match, the two equations describe one line, not two.

This is the same distinction as the degenerate cases in AR.2.4: equal slopes with different intercepts gives no solution, equal slopes with equal intercepts gives infinitely many.

---

##### Convert Before You Compare

Slope rules apply to **slopes**, so an equation not in $y = mx + b$ form has to be converted first.

**Example 1:** Are $2x + 4y = 8$ and $x + 2y = 10$ parallel?

Comparing the printed coefficients gives nothing useful; 2, 4, 8 and 1, 2, 10 look unrelated. Convert both.

- $4y = -2x + 8$, so $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 2$
- $2y = -x + 10$, so $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 5$

Same slope, different intercepts, so they are **parallel**.

**Example 2:** Are $3x - y = 6$ and $6x - 2y = 12$ parallel?

- $-y = -3x + 6$, so $y = 3x - 6$
- $-2y = -6x + 12$, so $y = 3x - 6$

Identical. The second equation is the first multiplied by 2, so this is one line written two ways, not a parallel pair.

**Never judge from the printed coefficients.** Convert both, then compare the slopes, then compare the intercepts.

---

##### Building the Equation

**Example 3:** Write the line through $(4, 1)$ parallel to $y = 5x - 2$.

- Parallel, so the slope is 5.
- Substitute the point: $1 = 5(4) + b$, so $b = -19$.
- $y = 5x - 19$

Check: $5(4) - 19 = 1$. Correct. Note that $y = 5x - 2$ itself passes through nothing of the sort, and answering with the original equation gives the same line rather than a parallel one.

**Example 4:** Write the line through $(6, 2)$ perpendicular to $y = 3x + 4$.

- Perpendicular, so the slope is $-\frac{1}{3}$. Check: $3 \times -\frac{1}{3} = -1$.
- Substitute: $2 = -\frac{1}{3}(6) + b = -2 + b$, so $b = 4$.
- $y = -\frac{1}{3}x + 4$

Check: $-\frac{1}{3}(6) + 4 = 2$. Correct.

The intercept coming out as 4 here is a coincidence of the numbers, not a rule. It still had to be solved for.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Negating without flipping.** Perpendicular to slope 2 is $-\frac{1}{2}$, not $-2$.
2. **Flipping without negating.** $\frac{1}{2}$ is not perpendicular to 2 either. Multiply the slopes and check for $-1$.
3. **Comparing printed coefficients.** Convert both equations to $y = mx + b$ first.
4. **Calling one line two parallel lines.** Matching slope **and** intercept means it is the same line.

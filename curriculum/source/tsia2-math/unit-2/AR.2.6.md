# AR.2.6 - Slope, Slope-Intercept Form, and Writing Equations of Lines

**Topic ID:** AR.2.6  
**Unit:** 2  
**Strand:** AR (Algebraic Reasoning)  
**Assessment Layer:** CRC  
**Author:** Juan Dolores Oviedo  

---

#### **Learning Objectives**

- Calculate slope from two points as rise over run, and write a line's equation by solving for b rather than copying a point's y-value.
- Write equations of horizontal and vertical lines by identifying which coordinate stays constant.
- Rearrange a standard-form equation into slope-intercept form by dividing every term by the same coefficient.

---

#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Two Numbers Describe Any Line

$$y = mx + b$$

$m$ is the **slope**, how far $y$ moves for each 1 unit of $x$. $b$ is the **y-intercept**, the value of $y$ when $x$ is zero. Fix those two and the line is completely determined.

You have met both already: $m$ is the rate of change from QR.3.2 and $b$ is the starting value. This topic is about producing them and writing them in the standard form.

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

You use a point's $y$ value as the intercept.

Write the equation of the line through $(2, 7)$ with slope 3.

Students write $y = 3x + 7$. The 7 came from the point, and the intercept slot wants the $y$ value at $x = 0$, not the $y$ value at $x = 2$.

Find $b$ properly by substituting the point into the form and solving:

$$7 = 3(2) + b \quad \rightarrow \quad 7 = 6 + b \quad \rightarrow \quad b = 1$$

$$y = 3x + 1$$

Check by putting the point back in: $3(2) + 1 = 7$. Correct. The wrong version gives $3(2) + 7 = 13$, which misses the point it was built from by 6.

**A given point is a constraint to solve with, not a number to copy into a slot.** The only time the $y$ value is the intercept is when the point's $x$ is zero.

##### Slope From Two Points

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

Through $(2, 3)$ and $(6, 11)$:

$$m = \frac{11 - 3}{6 - 2} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$$

Three ways to get this wrong, and all three are just failures to finish the division:

- Reporting **8**, the change in $y$ alone.
- Reporting **4**, the change in $x$ alone.
- Reporting $\frac{4}{8} = 0.5$, the run over the rise instead of the rise over the run.

**Rise over run.** The output's change goes on top. This is the same discipline as QR.3.6, and the same fix works: name the units, or at least name which quantity you divided by which.

---

##### Building the Whole Equation From Two Points

**Example 1:** Through $(2, 5)$ and $(6, 17)$.

- Slope: $\frac{17 - 5}{6 - 2} = \frac{12}{4} = 3$
- Intercept: substitute either point. $5 = 3(2) + b$, so $b = -1$
- $y = 3x - 1$

Check **both** points, which catches every version of this error at once: $3(2) - 1 = 5$ and $3(6) - 1 = 17$. Both reproduced.

---

##### Reading the Form

For $y = -3x + 7$: the slope is $-3$ and the intercept is $7$.

The sign belongs to the slope, so a line falling three units per step is $-3$, not 3. And the two numbers are not interchangeable: reporting slope 7 and intercept $-3$ describes a different line entirely.

Also note what a finished answer looks like. "$m = 4$" is a slope, not an equation. If the question asks for the equation of a line, the answer has an $=$ with $y$ on one side and something involving $x$ on the other.

---

##### Horizontal and Vertical Lines

These two get confused constantly and the fix is to think about what stays constant.

**Horizontal line through $(3, -2)$.** A horizontal line never rises, so its slope is 0 and every point on it has the same $y$. The equation is

$$y = -2$$

**Vertical line through $(4, 9)$.** A vertical line never runs, so its slope would need a division by zero and is **undefined**. Every point on it has the same $x$:

$$x = 4$$

| Line | Slope | Equation | What stays fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| horizontal | $0$ | $y = c$ | the $y$ value |
| vertical | undefined | $x = c$ | the $x$ value |

**Ask which coordinate never changes, and set that one equal to its value.** Walk along a horizontal line and $y$ never moves, so $y$ is what gets pinned.

---

##### Rearranging Into Slope-Intercept Form

**Example 2:** Write $2x + 3y = 12$ in slope-intercept form.

Isolate $y$, and divide **every** term by the coefficient.

- $3y = -2x + 12$
- $y = -\frac{2}{3}x + 4$

Both terms on the right were divided by 3. Dividing only the $x$ term leaves $y = -\frac{2}{3}x + 12$, which crosses the y-axis in the wrong place.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Copying a point's $y$ into the intercept.** Substitute and solve for $b$, then check the point.
2. **Reporting the rise or the run instead of the slope.** Finish the division, rise over run.
3. **Swapping $m$ and $b$, or dropping the slope's sign.** The number touching $x$ is the slope.
4. **Mixing up horizontal and vertical.** Whichever coordinate never changes is the one set equal to a constant.

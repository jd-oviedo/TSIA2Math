#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Two Questions That Are Not the Same Question

Put two linear models side by side and there are two entirely different things you can ask.

- **Which one is bigger right now?** That is about the starting values.
- **Which one is growing faster?** That is about the rates.

These have different answers, and the test builds items on people who blur them.

Plan A costs $C = 30 + 10m$. Plan B costs $C = 60 + 5m$.

| | starting value | rate |
|---|---|---|
| Plan A | \$30 | \$10 per month |
| Plan B | \$60 | \$5 per month |

Plan A starts cheaper. Plan B grows slower. **Neither one is simply "better."** Which is cheaper depends on how many months you are asking about, and that is the whole topic.

**Decide which of the two questions is being asked before you look at any numbers.**

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

You assume the one that starts ahead stays ahead.

Plan A starts at \$30 and Plan B at \$60, so Plan A looks like the obvious choice. At 3 months it is: $30 + 30 = 60$ against $60 + 15 = 75$.

Now run it out to 10 months.

- Plan A: $30 + 10(10) = 130$
- Plan B: $60 + 5(10) = 110$

Plan B is now cheaper by \$20. The cheaper starting value lost, because the faster rate ate the head start.

**A starting advantage is fixed. A rate advantage grows without limit.** Given enough of the input, the faster rate always wins, no matter how big the head start was. So "which is cheaper" is never answerable without a specific input value, and an item that gives you one is telling you it matters.

---

##### Where They Cross

The crossover is the input value where the two models are equal. Set the expressions equal and solve.

$$30 + 10m = 60 + 5m$$
$$10m - 5m = 60 - 30$$
$$5m = 30$$
$$m = 6$$

At 6 months both cost $30 + 60 = 90$. Check the other one: $60 + 30 = 90$. Agrees.

##### Crossing Once, Not Forever

At 6 months the plans cost the same. What happens at 7 months?

- Plan A: $30 + 70 = 100$
- Plan B: $60 + 35 = 95$

They separate again, with Plan B now ahead permanently. **Two straight lines with different slopes cross exactly once.** The crossover is a single moment, not a new equilibrium, and after it the faster-rising model stays higher forever.

Reading a crossover as "they are the same from now on" is the error the test is checking for. If the rates differ, equality lasts for exactly one input value.

---

##### The Gap Between Rates

"How much faster is A rising than B?" is answered by subtracting the rates:

$$10 - 5 = 5 \text{ dollars per month}$$

That \$5 per month is the speed at which the gap between them changes. It is why the \$30 head start disappears in exactly 6 months: $30 \div 5 = 6$.

**Adding the rates answers nothing.** \$15 a month is not a quantity in this problem.

---

##### Comparing Rates in Different Forms

Rates are often given as totals over different spans, and they have to be normalised before they can be compared, exactly as in QR.3.2.

**Example 1:** Runner X covers 12 km in 1.5 hours. Runner Y covers 21 km in 3 hours. Runner Z covers 9 km in 1 hour. Who is fastest?

- X: $12 \div 1.5 = 8$ km/h
- Y: $21 \div 3 = 7$ km/h
- Z: $9 \div 1 = 9$ km/h

Z is fastest. Note that Y covered the greatest distance and is the slowest of the three, which is exactly the trap: **a bigger total is not a bigger rate.**

And a comparison is always possible once both are per the same unit. Answering "these cannot be compared because the times differ" is giving up one division too early.

---

##### Both Linear Does Not Mean Both Equal

**Example 2:** Two accounts both grow linearly. Account 1 goes from \$200 to \$320 in 4 months. Account 2 goes from \$150 to \$330 in 5 months.

- Account 1: $\frac{320 - 200}{4} = 30$ per month
- Account 2: $\frac{330 - 150}{5} = 36$ per month

Both are linear and their rates differ. "Linear" means the rate is constant *within* each model, and says nothing about how two models compare to each other.

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Assuming the leader stays the leader.** A head start is fixed; a rate advantage compounds. Always evaluate at the input the question names.
2. **Comparing starting values when the question asked about rates.** Decide which question is being asked first.
3. **Adding the rates instead of subtracting.** The crossover uses the difference, both of rates and of starting values.
4. **Reading a crossover as permanent.** Two lines with different slopes are equal at exactly one input.

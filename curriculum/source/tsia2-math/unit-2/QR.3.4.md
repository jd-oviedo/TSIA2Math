#### **Part 1: Guided Notes**

##### Saying What the Numbers Mean

QR.3.2 asked you to point at the rate. This topic asks you to say what it **means** in the situation, in a sentence, with units.

That sounds softer than arithmetic. It is not. These items are nearly all multiple choice between four English sentences that differ by one word, and the wrong ones are wrong for reasons you can name.

A plumber charges $C = 15h + 40$, where $h$ is hours.

| Number | Name | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| $15$ | slope, rate of change | "the cost goes up \$15 for each additional hour" |
| $40$ | intercept, starting value | "the cost is \$40 before any hours are worked" |

Two words carry all the weight. The slope sentence needs **"for each"** or **"per."** The intercept sentence needs **"before any"** or **"when the input is zero."**

**If your sentence has no "per" in it, you have not described a rate.**

---

##### The Mistake That Costs the Most Points

You attach the intercept to the wrong moment.

A phone plan is $C = 0.10m + 20$, where $m$ is minutes. Students write: "the \$20 covers the first 20 minutes."

It does not, and the number 20 appearing twice in that sentence is a coincidence the item is built on. The \$20 is what you pay at **zero** minutes. The very first minute costs \$0.10 on top of it, so 20 minutes costs $0.10(20) + 20 = 22$.

The intercept is tied to one input value and only one: **zero**. Not the first item, not the first twenty, not "at the start of the month if you use it a bit." Zero.

**Substitute zero and read what comes out.** $C = 0.10(0) + 20 = 20$. That is the entire meaning of the intercept, and it is a two-second check.

---

##### The Three Ways to Misname a Number

Almost every wrong option in this topic is one of three moves. Learn to spot them and the items become fast.

**1. Swapping slope and intercept.** "The starting cost is \$15 and it rises \$40 per hour." Both numbers are present, both in the wrong role. Fix: the slope is the one touching the variable.

**2. Calling a rate a total.** "The job costs \$15." A rate is not a total; it is a total *per something*. If the sentence could be true for only one particular input, it is not describing a rate.

**3. Reading the coefficient as a count of the variable.** "The job takes 15 hours." The 15 is dollars per hour, not hours. Fix: attach units. Dollars per hour cannot be a number of hours.

**Attach units to every candidate sentence.** The wrong ones stop making sense immediately.

---

##### Negative Slopes Say "Down"

$W = 180 - 2d$ gives a weight in pounds after $d$ weeks.

The slope is $-2$, and the sentence is "the weight goes down 2 pounds each week." Reporting "goes up 2 pounds" keeps the magnitude and loses the meaning, which is the whole point of the item.

**Example 1:** $D = 250 - 55t$ gives miles remaining after $t$ hours of driving.

- $250$: miles remaining before the drive starts.
- $-55$: the distance remaining falls by 55 miles each hour, so the car travels at 55 miles per hour.
- The trip ends when $D = 0$: $250 = 55t$, so $t = 250 \div 55 \approx 4.55$ hours.

Notice that the slope's sign is about the *quantity being modelled*, not about the car. Miles remaining goes down; miles travelled goes up. Same drive, opposite signs, because they measure different things.

---

##### Interpreting Two Models Side by Side

**Example 2:** Car A is worth $V = 24000 - 1800t$ and Car B is worth $V = 19000 - 1200t$, after $t$ years.

- Car A starts higher: \$24,000 against \$19,000. That is the intercept.
- Car A falls faster: \$1,800 a year against \$1,200. That is the slope.

Those are two separate comparisons and the test likes to swap them. "Car A loses value faster" is about the slopes. "Car A is worth more today" is about the intercepts. **Decide which one the question is asking about before you look at the numbers.**

---

##### The Four Traps

1. **Tying the intercept to a non-zero input.** The \$20 is the cost at zero minutes, not the cost of the first 20 minutes. Substitute zero.
2. **Swapping slope and intercept.** The slope is the number touching the variable.
3. **Calling a rate a total.** A rate sentence needs a "per" in it.
4. **Losing the sign.** A negative slope means the modelled quantity goes down.

# **Lesson 8.1: Understanding Exponential Functions**

## **Why Should You Care About Exponential Functions?**

Exponential functions model **rapid growth and decay** found in real life—whether it’s **population growth, viral outbreaks, or radioactive decay**. Understanding these functions helps you predict and analyze change more accurately.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **identify exponential growth and decay and apply these concepts to real-world problems.**

---

## **1. What Are Exponential Functions?**

An **exponential function** follows this form:

$y = a \cdot b^x$

where:

* **$a$** = initial amount (starting value)
* **$b$** = growth or decay factor
* **$x$** = exponent (time, number of periods, etc.)

---

## **2. Identifying Exponential Growth vs. Decay**

🔹 **Exponential Growth** occurs when a quantity **increases** over time.  
✅ If $b > 1$, the function represents **growth**.

🔹 **Exponential Decay** occurs when a quantity **decreases** over time.  
✅ If $0 < b < 1$, the function represents **decay**.

📌 **Key Differences:**

| Type | Formula | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Growth** | $y = a \cdot b^x$ (**$b > 1$**) | Curve moves **upward**, values increase rapidly |
| **Decay** | $y = a \cdot b^x$ (**$0 < b < 1$**) | Curve moves **downward**, values decrease over time |

🔹 **Graph Features of Exponential Functions:**  
✅ The **y-values** increase or decrease **rapidly**.  
✅ The graph has a **horizontal asymptote** (approaches but never touches the x-axis).  
✅ The function is always **positive** if $a > 0$.

---

## **3. Exponential Growth in Real Life**

**Exponential growth** models situations where something **doubles, triples, or increases by a constant percentage** over time.

📊 **Example 1: Population Growth**

A city’s population starts at 50,000 and grows by **5% per year**.

$P(t) = 50000(1.05)^t$

* **$a = 50,000$** (starting population)
* **$b = 1.05$** (growth factor: 1 + 0.05)
* **$t$ = years**

🔹 **Predict the population in 10 years:**

$P(10) = 50000(1.05)^{10} = 81,445$

✅ The population will grow to **81,445** in 10 years!

---

## **4. Exponential Decay in Real Life**

**Exponential decay** models situations where something **shrinks by a constant percentage** over time.

📉 **Example 2: Radioactive Decay**

A scientist has **100g of a substance** that decays by **20% per hour**.

$M(t) = 100(0.8)^t$

* **$a = 100$** (initial mass)
* **$b = 0.8$** (decay factor: 1 - 0.2)
* **$t$ = hours**

🔹 **Find the mass after 5 hours:**

$M(5) = 100(0.8)^5 = 32.8$

✅ After 5 hours, only **32.8g** of the substance remains.

---

## **5. Key Steps to Identify Exponential Functions**

✅ Look for a **multiplicative pattern** (values multiply by a constant factor, not add).  
✅ Check if the function follows **$y = a \cdot b^x$** form.  
✅ Identify **$b$**:

* If $b > 1$ → **Growth**
* If $0 < b < 1$ → **Decay**  
  ✅ Graph the function and check for a **curve** (not a straight line).

---

## **6. Applications and Predictions Using Exponential Models**

🔹 **Finance**: Compound interest in bank accounts  
🔹 **Biology**: Bacteria growth and virus spread  
🔹 **Physics**: Radioactive decay and half-life  
🔹 **Technology**: Spread of trends and viral content

✅ **Exponential models help us predict trends and make data-driven decisions!**

---

## **7. Final Thoughts**

Exponential functions are **powerful tools** for modeling rapid change. Whether it’s **growth** (money, populations, infections) or **decay** (radioactivity, cooling, depreciation), these functions allow us to **predict and understand** real-world phenomena.

🚀 **Mastering exponential functions helps you make informed decisions in science, business, and everyday life!**

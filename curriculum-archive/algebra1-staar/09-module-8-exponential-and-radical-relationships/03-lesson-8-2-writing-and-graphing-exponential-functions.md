# **Lesson 8.2: Writing and Graphing Exponential Functions**

## **Why Should You Care About Exponential vs. Linear Functions?**

Understanding **exponential functions** helps you analyze situations where change **accelerates** or **decreases rapidly** over time. Comparing them to **linear functions** helps you recognize when exponential models are the better choice in real-world applications like **finance, population growth, and technology trends**.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **write and graph exponential functions and compare them to linear functions.**

---

## **1. What Are Exponential Functions?**

An **exponential function** follows this form:

$y = a \cdot b^x$

where:

* **$a$** = initial value (starting amount)
* **$b$** = growth or decay factor
* **$x$** = exponent (time, periods, etc.)

### **Exponential vs. Linear Growth**

A **linear function** follows the form:

$y = mx + b$

where:

* **$m$** = constant rate of change (slope)
* **$b$** = y-intercept (starting value)

📌 **Key Difference:**  
🔹 **Linear functions** increase/decrease by a constant **amount** (addition/subtraction).  
🔹 **Exponential functions** increase/decrease by a constant **factor** (multiplication).

📊 **Example: Population Growth**

* A city gains **500 people per year** → **Linear growth** $P(t) = 500t + 10000$
* A city grows by **5% per year** → **Exponential growth** $P(t) = 10000(1.05)^t$

**🔑 Key takeaway:** Linear growth is steady, while exponential growth starts slow but **increases rapidly** over time.

---

## **2. Writing Exponential Functions from Word Problems**

To **write an exponential function**, identify:  
✅ **Initial value $a$** = starting amount  
✅ **Growth/decay factor $b$**:

* If **growth**, $b > 1$
* If **decay**, $0 < b < 1$  
  ✅ **Variable $x$** represents time or periods

📉 **Example 1: Exponential Decay**  
A car’s value is **$30,000**, and it **loses 15% of its value per year**.

$V(t) = 30000(0.85)^t$

🔹 **Why $0.85$?** Since the car loses **15%**, the decay factor is $1 - 0.15 = 0.85$.

🔹 **Find the value after 5 years:**

$V(5) = 30000(0.85)^5 = 13278.76$

✅ After **5 years**, the car is worth **$13,278.76**.

---

## **3. Graphing Exponential vs. Linear Functions**

📊 **Key Features of Exponential Graphs:**  
✅ **Curved** shape (not straight like linear graphs)  
✅ **Horizontal asymptote** (approaches but never touches the x-axis)  
✅ **Steep increase (growth) or gradual decrease (decay)**

📉 **Example 2: Comparing Growth Rates**

| Year | Linear Growth ($y = 2x + 1$) | Exponential Growth ($y = 2^x$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 2 | 5 | 4 |
| 3 | 7 | 8 |
| 4 | 9 | 16 |

🔹 **Graphing both functions:**

* The **linear function** forms a **straight** line.
* The **exponential function** starts lower but **quickly surpasses** the linear function!

🚀 **Exponential functions eventually dominate linear functions in long-term growth.**

---

## **4. Applications of Exponential and Linear Models**

🔹 **When to Use a Linear Model?**  
✅ **Constant change** (salary increases by $2,000 per year, car drives 60 miles per hour)  
✅ **Predictable, steady growth**

🔹 **When to Use an Exponential Model?**  
✅ **Growth by a percentage** (population, investments, bacteria growth)  
✅ **Decay over time** (radioactive material, depreciation, cooling of objects)

📊 **Example 3: Bank Account Growth**  
You deposit **$1,000** into a savings account with **4% annual interest**.

$A(t) = 1000(1.04)^t$

🔹 After **10 years**, your savings grow to:

$A(10) = 1000(1.04)^{10} = 1,480.24$

✅ **Exponential growth makes a BIG difference over time!**

---

## **5. Key Takeaways**

🔹 **Linear functions** grow at a constant rate (addition).  
🔹 **Exponential functions** grow at an increasing rate (multiplication).  
🔹 **Exponential growth surpasses linear growth over time** in real-world situations.  
🔹 **Graphing both functions helps visualize their differences.**

📌 **Mastering exponential functions allows you to analyze finance, science, and technology trends effectively!** 🚀

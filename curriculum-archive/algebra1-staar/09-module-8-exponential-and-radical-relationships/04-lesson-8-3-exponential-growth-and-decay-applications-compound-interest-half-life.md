# **Lesson 8.3: Exponential Growth and Decay**

## **Why Should You Care About Exponential Growth and Decay?**

Exponential functions model real-world situations like **population growth, radioactive decay, and compound interest.** Understanding **how things grow or decline exponentially** helps in fields like **finance, science, and business.**

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **apply exponential functions to half-life problems and compound interest calculations.**

---

## **1. Exponential Growth vs. Exponential Decay**

An **exponential function** follows the general form:

$y = a \cdot b^x$

where:

* **$a$** = initial value (starting amount)
* **$b$** = growth or decay factor
* **$x$** = time or number of periods

📌 **Key Differences:**

* If **$b > 1$** → **Exponential Growth** (population, investments)
* If **$0 < b < 1$** → **Exponential Decay** (radioactive decay, depreciation)

📊 **Example: Bacteria Growth vs. Drug Decay**

* A bacteria culture **doubles** every hour → **Growth** $N(t) = 100(2)^t$
* A drug’s concentration **decreases by 25%** per hour → **Decay** $C(t) = 200(0.75)^t$

---

## **2. Understanding Half-Life**

📌 **What is Half-Life?**  
Half-life is the time it takes for **half** of a substance to decay. The formula for half-life decay is:

$A(t) = A\_0 \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{t}{h}}$

where:

* **$A\_0$** = initial amount
* **$h$** = half-life period
* **$t$** = time elapsed

📉 **Example 1: Radioactive Decay**  
A **100g** sample of carbon-14 has a half-life of **5,730 years**. How much remains after **17,190 years**?

$A(17190) = 100 \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{17190}{5730}}$ $A(17190) = 100 \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{3}$ $A(17190) = 100 \times \frac{1}{8} = 12.5g$

✅ After **17,190 years**, only **12.5g** of carbon-14 remains.

## **3. Understanding Compound Interest**

📌 **What is Compound Interest?**  
Compound interest is when interest **accumulates on both the principal and previously earned interest.**

$A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$

where:

* **$A$** = final amount
* **$P$** = initial deposit (principal)
* **$r$** = annual interest rate (decimal form)
* **$n$** = number of times interest is compounded per year
* **$t$** = number of years

📈 **Example 2: Investing in a Savings Account**  
You invest **$5,000** in a bank offering **6% annual interest**, compounded **monthly**. How much will you have after **10 years**?

$A = 5000 \left(1 + \frac{0.06}{12}\right)^{12(10)}$ $A = 5000 \left(1.005\right)^{120}$ $A \approx 5000(1.8194) = 9,097$

✅ After **10 years**, your investment grows to **$9,097**.

📌 **The more frequently interest is compounded, the faster the growth!**

## **4. Graphing Exponential Growth and Decay**

📊 **Key Features of Growth and Decay Graphs:**  
✅ **Growth graphs** rise **steeply** over time (e.g., investments, populations).  
✅ **Decay graphs** decrease and **approach zero** (e.g., radioactive decay, drug elimination).  
✅ **Both graphs have a horizontal asymptote**, meaning values never reach absolute zero.

📈 **Example 3: Comparing Growth and Decay**

| Year | Growth ($y = 2^x$) | Decay ($y = (0.5)^x$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 2 | 0.5 |
| 2 | 4 | 0.25 |
| 3 | 8 | 0.125 |

🔹 **Growth accelerates**, while **decay slows down** but never reaches zero.

## **5. Applications of Exponential Growth and Decay**

🔹 **Where Do You See Exponential Growth?**  
✅ **Investments and interest rates**  
✅ **Population growth**  
✅ **Bacteria and virus spread**

🔹 **Where Do You See Exponential Decay?**  
✅ **Radioactive decay and half-life**  
✅ **Car depreciation**  
✅ **Medicine elimination in the body**

📊 **Example 4: Caffeine in Your System**  
If a cup of coffee has **120 mg of caffeine** and it has a half-life of **5 hours**, how much remains after **10 hours**?

$C(10) = 120 \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{10}{5}}$ $C(10) = 120 \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^2$ $C(10) = 120 \times \frac{1}{4} = 30 mg$

✅ After **10 hours**, only **30 mg** of caffeine remains in your system.

## **6. Key Takeaways**

🔹 **Exponential Growth** happens when a quantity increases by a percentage over time (**b > 1**).  
🔹 **Exponential Decay** happens when a quantity decreases by a percentage over time (**0 < b < 1**).  
🔹 **Half-life formula** models decay in substances like **radioactive materials and medication elimination**.  
🔹 **Compound interest formula** helps predict financial growth over time.  
🔹 **Exponential functions appear everywhere in science, business, and nature.**

📌 **Mastering exponential growth and decay helps you make smart financial decisions and understand scientific phenomena!** 🚀

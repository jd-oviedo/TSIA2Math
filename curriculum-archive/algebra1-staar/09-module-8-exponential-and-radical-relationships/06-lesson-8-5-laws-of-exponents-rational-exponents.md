# **Lesson 8.5: Laws of Exponents**

## **Why Should You Care About Exponent Rules?**

Exponents are **everywhere** in math and science—whether you’re calculating **growth rates, physics formulas, or computer algorithms**. Understanding the **laws of exponents** makes solving complex expressions faster and easier! Exponents aren’t just whole numbers! **Fractional exponents (rational exponents)** are another way to express roots, and **negative exponents** help us rewrite expressions in a more convenient form.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **convert between radical and exponent notation,** simplify expressions with **rational and integral exponents,** and **apply exponent rules to simplify expressions and solve problems efficiently**!

---

## **1. What Are Exponents?**

An **exponent** tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.

$a^n = a \times a \times a \times \dots \times a \quad (n \text{ times})$

📌 **Key Terms:**

* **Base**: The number being multiplied (e.g., **$a$** in **$a^n$**)
* **Exponent**: The small raised number (e.g., **$n$** in **$a^n$**)

🔹 **Example:**

$2^5 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 32$

---

## **2. The Laws of Exponents**

### **Law #1: Product Rule**

$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$

🔹 **Example:**

$x^3 \times x^4 = x^{3+4} = x^7$

📌 **Key Tip:** Multiply **same bases** by **adding exponents**!

---

### **Law #2: Quotient Rule**

$\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}, \quad (a \neq 0)$

🔹 **Example:**

$\frac{y^6}{y^2} = y^{6-2} = y^4$

📌 **Key Tip:** Divide **same bases** by **subtracting exponents**!

---

### **Law #3: Power Rule**

$(a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}$

🔹 **Example:**

$(3^2)^4 = 3^{2 \times 4} = 3^8 = 6,561$

📌 **Key Tip:** Raising a power to a power? **Multiply** exponents!

---

### **Law #4: Power of a Product Rule**

$(ab)^n = a^n \times b^n$

🔹 **Example:**

$(2x)^3 = 2^3 \times x^3 = 8x^3$

📌 **Key Tip:** Distribute the exponent **to each factor** inside parentheses!

---

### **Law #5: Power of a Quotient Rule**

$\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}, \quad (b \neq 0)$

🔹 **Example:**

$\left(\frac{4}{x}\right)^2 = \frac{4^2}{x^2} = \frac{16}{x^2}$

📌 **Key Tip:** Apply the exponent to **both numerator and denominator**!

---

### **Law #6: Zero Exponent Rule**

$a^0 = 1, \quad (a \neq 0)$

🔹 **Example:**

$7^0 = 1, \quad x^0 = 1$

📌 **Key Tip:** Any nonzero number **raised to the power of 0 is always 1**!

---

### **Law #7: Negative Exponent Rule**

$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}, \quad (a \neq 0)$

🔹 **Example:**

$5^{-3} = \frac{1}{5^3} = \frac{1}{125}$

📌 **Key Tip:** A **negative exponent means take the reciprocal**!

---

## **3. Applying Exponent Rules to Simplify Expressions**

Let’s put these rules into action!

🔹 **Example 1: Simplify $(x^2 y^3)(x^5 y)$**

$= x^{2+5} y^{3+1}$ $= x^7 y^4$

🔹 **Example 2: Simplify $\frac{a^6 b^4}{a^2 b^3}$**

$= a^{6-2} b^{4-3}$ $= a^4 b^1 = a^4 b$

🔹 **Example 3: Simplify $(3x^2 y^3)^4$**

$= 3^4 x^{2 \times 4} y^{3 \times 4}$ $= 81x^8 y^{12}$

📌 **Key Tip:** Break down the problem step by step using the **laws of exponents**!

## **1. What Are Rational and Integral Exponents?**

🔹 **Integral Exponents**: Exponents that are whole numbers (positive or negative).  
🔹 **Rational Exponents**: Exponents that are fractions.

**Exponent Notation**:

$a^m \quad (\text{Integral exponent})$ $a^{\frac{m}{n}} \quad (\text{Rational exponent})$

📌 **Key Idea:** **Fractional exponents represent roots!**

---

## **2. Converting Between Radical and Exponent Notation**

### **Rational Exponents as Roots**

$a^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a}$

🔹 **Example:**

$16^{\frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{16} = 4$ $27^{\frac{1}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{27} = 3$

📌 **Key Tip:** A denominator of **$n$** in an exponent means **$n$th root**!

---

### **Rational Exponents with Powers**

$a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$

🔹 **Example:**

$8^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left(\sqrt[3]{8}\right)^2 = 2^2 = 4$ $32^{\frac{3}{5}} = \left(\sqrt[5]{32}\right)^3 = 2^3 = 8$

📌 **Key Tip:** **Solve the root first, then apply the power!**

---

## **4. Key Takeaways**

✅ **Exponents make repeated multiplication easy!**  
✅ **Use exponent rules to simplify expressions quickly.**  
✅ **Negative exponents mean reciprocals, and zero exponents always equal 1.**

📌 **Mastering exponent rules helps in algebra, calculus, and real-world applications like computing, engineering, and physics! 🚀**

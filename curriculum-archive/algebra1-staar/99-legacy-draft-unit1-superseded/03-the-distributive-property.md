**Lesson 1.3: The Distributive Property**  
**Objective:** Use the Distributive Property to evaluate and simplify expressions.  
**TEKS:** A.10(D) Rewrite polynomial expressions of degree one and degree two in equivalent forms using the distributive property.

---

### 🚀 Why This Matters

The distributive property is like a shortcut for multiplying through parentheses. You’ll use it all year long — especially when solving equations or simplifying expressions. On the STAAR, questions often hide this property inside bigger problems, so mastering it now will save you from confusion later.

---

### 🧠 Vocabulary You Need to Know

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Coefficient** | The number being multiplied by a variable | In `3x`, the coefficient is **3** |
| **Like Terms** | Terms that have the same variable and exponent | `4x` and `-2x` are like terms |
| **Simplest Form** | When all like terms are combined and parentheses are removed | `2x + 3x` becomes `5x` |

---

### 💥 The Distributive Property Rule

When a number (or variable) is **outside parentheses**, multiply it by **everything inside**.  
  
![LaTeX: a\left(b+c\right)=ab+ac](https://k12.instructure.com/equation_images/a%255Cleft(b%252Bc%255Cright)%253Dab%252Bac?scale=1 "a\left(b+c\right)=ab+ac")  
  
You “*distribute*” the outside value to each term inside.

---

### 🔢 Step-by-Step Examples

**Example 1:**  
Simplify `3(4 + 2)`

* Multiply `3 × 4 = 12`
* Multiply `3 × 2 = 6`
* Add: `12 + 6 = 18`  
  ✅ **Final Answer:** `18`

---

**Example 2:**  
Simplify `5(x + 3)`

* Multiply `5 × x = 5x`
* Multiply `5 × 3 = 15`  
  ✅ **Expression:** `5x + 15`

This means if you know the value of `x`, you can plug it in and evaluate it.

---

**Example 3:**  
Simplify `2(3x + 4) + 6`

* Distribute: `2 × 3x = 6x` and `2 × 4 = 8` → `6x + 8 + 6`
* Combine like terms: `6x + 14`  
  ✅ **Simplified Expression:** `6x + 14`

---

**Example 4 (With Negative Signs):**  
Simplify `-4(2x - 3)`

* Multiply `-4 × 2x = -8x`
* Multiply `-4 × (-3) = +12`  
  ✅ **Answer:** `-8x + 12`

⚠️ **Watch Out:** Two negatives make a positive! Don’t lose that sign.

---

### 💬 Real-Life Examples

**Money Example:**  
You’re buying 3 concert tickets that each cost `(25 + tax)` dollars.  
Expression: `3(25 + tax)`  
Distribute → `75 + 3tax`  
This shows your **total cost** is the base price plus tax for all tickets.

---

**Family Example:**  
Your mom gives each of your 4 cousins `$5` plus `$2` extra for snacks.  
Expression: `4(5 + 2)`  
Distribute → `20 + 8 = 28`  
✅ Everyone’s happy — and you understand distribution.

---

### 🧩 Practice with Variables

**Example 5:**  
Simplify `7(a + 2b – 3)`

* Distribute:

  + `7 × a = 7a`
  + `7 × 2b = 14b`
  + `7 × (-3) = -21`  
    ✅ Expression: `7a + 14b - 21`

---

**Example 6:**  
Simplify and evaluate `4(x + 5)` when `x = 2`

1. Distribute: `4x + 20`
2. Substitute: `4(2) + 20 = 8 + 20 = 28`  
   ✅ **Value:** `28`

---

### 🎯 STAAR Tip

1. **Look for parentheses first.** STAAR loves hiding them in word problems.
2. **Always distribute before combining like terms.**
3. **Be careful with negatives!** The sign in front of parentheses applies to *everything* inside.

   * Example: `–(x + 2)` → `–x – 2`
4. **Simplify completely.** STAAR answers often include unsimplified expressions to trick you.

---

### ❌ Common Mistakes

* Forgetting to multiply by **every** term inside the parentheses.
* Losing the negative sign in front of parentheses.
* Stopping before combining like terms.

---

### 🧭 Quick Recap

* Multiply what’s **outside** the parentheses by **everything inside**.
* Combine **like terms** to simplify.
* Watch your **signs** — negatives are sneaky.
* The distributive property helps you rewrite, simplify, and evaluate expressions quickly — a skill STAAR expects you to master.

**Lesson 1.2: Order of Operations**  
**Objective:** Evaluate numerical and algebraic expressions by using the order of operations.  
**TEKS:** A.5(A) Solve linear equations in one variable, including those for which the application of the distributive property is necessary and for which variables are included on both sides.

---

### 🚀 Why This Matters

The order of operations keeps everyone on the same page when solving math problems. Imagine if everyone did math in their own way—one person adds first, another multiplies first—no one would ever agree on an answer.  
This lesson is about following the universal rule that helps you get the *right* answer every time.

---

### 🧠 Vocabulary You Need to Know

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Evaluate** | To find the value of an expression | Evaluate `3 + 4 × 2` → `11` |
| **Order of Operations** | The specific order you must follow when simplifying expressions | PEMDAS |
| **Exponent** | The small number that tells how many times to multiply the base by itself | In `3²`, the exponent is 2 |
| **Base** | The number that is being multiplied | In `3²`, the base is 3 |

---

### 🔢 The Rule: **PEMDAS**

Say it with me: **Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally**  
It’s a memory trick for remembering the order:

1. **P** – Parentheses
2. **E** – Exponents
3. **M** – Multiplication
4. **D** – Division
5. **A** – Addition
6. **S** – Subtraction

⚠️ **Important:**  
Multiplication and Division are **equal in rank** – do them **from left to right**.  
Same for Addition and Subtraction — **left to right**, not “addition before subtraction.”

---

### 💬 Step-by-Step Examples

**Example 1:**

> Simplify `8 + 3 × (4 – 2)²`

**Step 1:** Parentheses → `(4 – 2) = 2`  
**Step 2:** Exponents → `2² = 4`  
**Step 3:** Multiplication → `3 × 4 = 12`  
**Step 4:** Addition → `8 + 12 = 20`  
✅ **Final Answer:** `20`

---

**Example 2:**

> Simplify `20 ÷ 5 × 2`

**Step 1:** Multiplication and Division are equal rank, so go **left to right**  
`20 ÷ 5 = 4`  
`4 × 2 = 8`  
✅ **Final Answer:** `8`

*(Common Mistake:)* Don’t do `5 × 2 = 10` first — that’s ignoring the left-to-right rule.

---

### 🧩 Evaluating Algebraic Expressions

Now, let’s add variables into the mix.

**Example 3:**

> Evaluate `3x² + 2x – 4` when `x = 3`

**Step 1:** Substitute → `3(3)² + 2(3) – 4`  
**Step 2:** Exponents → `3(9) + 6 – 4`  
**Step 3:** Multiplication → `27 + 6 – 4`  
**Step 4:** Addition/Subtraction → `29`  
✅ **Final Answer:** `29`

### 🎮 Real-Life Examples

**Money:**  
You’re shopping online. The cost of each hoodie is $25, there’s a $10 discount, and tax is 8%.  
Expression: `25 × h – 10 + 0.08(25 × h – 10)`  
Following the order of operations makes sure you calculate the **subtotal before tax**, not after.

---

**Sports:**  
You earn 2 points for every basket and 3 points for every three-pointer.  
Expression: `2b + 3t`  
If you made 4 baskets and 2 threes → `2(4) + 3(2)` = `8 + 6` = `14 points`

### 🎯 STAAR Tip

If a question asks you to simplify an expression, always:

1. **Circle or underline parentheses and exponents first.**
2. **Work step-by-step** — STAAR often gives you answer choices that match common mistakes.
3. **Double-check order:** Parentheses → Exponents → Multiplication/Division → Addition/Subtraction.
4. **Plug answers back in** if the problem involves substitution — this helps you verify quickly.

---

### ❌ Common Mistakes

* Doing addition before multiplication.
* Forgetting to apply exponents before multiplying.
* Mixing up “left to right” order for multiplication and division.
* Skipping substitution parentheses (always wrap substituted numbers in parentheses!).

---

### 🧭 Quick Recap

* PEMDAS keeps math fair and consistent.
* Always follow **Parentheses → Exponents → Multiply/Divide (left to right) → Add/Subtract (left to right)**.
* Substitute carefully and simplify step by step.
* Think logically — just like you wouldn’t put on your shoes before your socks, you shouldn’t add before multiplying.

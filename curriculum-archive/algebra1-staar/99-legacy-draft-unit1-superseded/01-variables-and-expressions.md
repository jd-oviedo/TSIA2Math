**Objective:** Write verbal expressions for algebraic expressions, and write algebraic expressions for verbal expressions.  
**TEKS:** A.2(C) Write linear equations in two variables given a table of values, a graph, and a verbal description.

---

### 🚀 Why This Matters

Algebra is the language of math. Once you learn how to translate words into math expressions, you’ll be able to understand and solve real problems — like figuring out how much you’ll spend, save, or earn. The STAAR loves testing this skill because it shows you actually *understand* what equations mean, not just how to punch numbers into a calculator.

---

### 🧠 Vocabulary You Need to Know

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Variable** | A letter that stands for an unknown number | `x`, `y`, or `n` |
| **Term** | A number, variable, or the product of both | `3x`, `-2y`, `5` |
| **Algebraic Expression** | A combination of numbers, variables, and operations (no equal sign) | `2x + 5` or `4n - 3` |

---

### 💬 Translating Words into Math

When you read math problems, it’s like decoding a message. Each phrase hints at a math operation:

| Verbal Phrase | Operation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| sum, increased by, more than, total | ➕ Addition | “5 more than x” → `x + 5` |
| difference, less than, decreased by | ➖ Subtraction | “7 less than n” → `n - 7` |
| product of, times, twice | ✖️ Multiplication | “Twice a number” → `2x` |
| quotient of, divided by, per | ➗ Division | “The quotient of a number and 3” → `x ÷ 3` or `x/3` |

**⚠️ Common Trap:**  
When you see “less than,” *flip the order!*

> “5 less than x” = `x - 5` (NOT `5 - x`)

---

### 💵 Real-Life Examples

**Example 1: Money**

> “Maria earns $12 per hour.”  
> Write an expression for how much she makes after *h* hours.  
> ✅ `12h`

If she gets a $20 tip, then total earnings = `12h + 20`

---

**Example 2: Sports**

> “A basketball player scored twice as many points as his teammate.”  
> If the teammate scored *p* points:  
> ✅ The player scored `2p`

---

**Example 3: Food**

> “The price of tacos is $2 each plus a $3 delivery fee.”  
> ✅ `2t + 3` (where *t* = number of tacos)

---

### ✍️ Translating Math Back Into Words

You’ll also see problems that start with the *expression* and ask you to write the *verbal phrase.*

| Algebraic Expression | Possible Verbal Expression |
| --- | --- |
| `x + 8` | “8 more than a number” |
| `4n` | “Four times a number” |
| `y - 3` | “A number decreased by 3” |
| `p ÷ 5` | “The quotient of a number and 5” |

---

### 🎯 STAAR Tip

When you see a word problem:

1. **Underline key words** that tell you the operation.
2. **Assign a variable** (like *x* for “a number”).
3. **Write the expression** step by step before rushing to solve.
4. Watch for **“less than”** and **“more than”** — those often flip the order!

### 💡 Practice Challenge

Write algebraic expressions for each:

1. A number increased by 9
2. 7 less than a number
3. Twice a number minus 4
4. Half of a number plus 6

**Bonus:** Write a real-life situation that could match one of your expressions.

---

### 🧩 Quick Recap

* Variables = letters that stand for unknowns.
* Expressions = math phrases with no equal sign.
* Pay attention to key words and order (especially with “less than”).
* Always connect the math to real life — that’s how you make sense of it.

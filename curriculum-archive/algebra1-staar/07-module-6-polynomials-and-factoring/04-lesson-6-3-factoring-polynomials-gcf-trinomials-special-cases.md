# **Lesson 6.3: Factoring Polynomials**

## **Why Should You Care About Factoring?**

Factoring is one of the most powerful tools in algebra! It’s like reverse engineering a problem to break it down into simpler parts. **Mastering factoring will help you solve equations faster, simplify expressions, and even understand real-world concepts like physics and economics.**

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **factor polynomials using different techniques**, including **GCF, grouping, special products, and trinomials**.

---

## **1. What Is Factoring?**

Factoring is the process of **rewriting a polynomial as a product of simpler polynomials**. Think of it like breaking a number down into its prime factors:

$12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$

In the same way, you can break a polynomial like $x^2 + 5x + 6$ into factors:

$(x + 2)(x + 3)$

Factoring makes solving equations much easier because **when a product equals zero, at least one of the factors must be zero**. This is called the **Zero Product Property**.

---

## **2. Factoring Methods**

### **(A) Factoring the Greatest Common Factor (GCF)**

The **first step in factoring any polynomial** is checking for a **GCF**—the largest expression that divides each term.

#### **Example 1: Factor $12x^3 + 18x^2 - 6x$**

📌 **Step 1: Find the GCF**

* The GCF of **12, 18, and 6** is **6**.
* The smallest power of $x$ in all terms is $x$, so we also factor out **$x$**.

📌 **Step 2: Factor out the GCF**

$12x^3 + 18x^2 - 6x = 6x(2x^2 + 3x - 1)$

✅ **Final Answer:**

$6x(2x^2 + 3x - 1)$

---

### **(B) Factoring by Grouping**

Grouping is useful when you have **four terms** in your polynomial.

#### **Example 2: Factor $x^3 + 3x^2 + 2x + 6$**

📌 **Step 1: Group the terms into two pairs**

$(x^3 + 3x^2) + (2x + 6)$

📌 **Step 2: Factor out the GCF from each group**

$x^2(x + 3) + 2(x + 3)$

📌 **Step 3: Factor out the common binomial factor $(x + 3)$**

$(x + 3)(x^2 + 2)$

✅ **Final Answer:**

$(x + 3)(x^2 + 2)$

---

### **(C) Factoring Trinomials**

Trinomials are polynomials with **three terms** in the form:

$ax^2 + bx + c$

There are **two main cases**:

#### **Case 1: Trinomials with $a = 1$ (Basic Case)**

**Example 3: Factor $x^2 + 7x + 12$**

📌 **Step 1: Find two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7**

* **$3$ and $4$ work because $3 \times 4 = 12$ and $3 + 4 = 7$**.

📌 **Step 2: Write the factors**

$(x + 3)(x + 4)$

✅ **Final Answer:**

$(x + 3)(x + 4)$

#### **Case 2: Trinomials with $a \neq 1$ (Advanced Case)**

**Example 4: Factor $2x^2 + 5x + 3$**

📌 **Step 1: Multiply $a$ and $c$**

* $2 \times 3 = 6$

📌 **Step 2: Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 5**

* **$2$ and $3$ work**.

📌 **Step 3: Rewrite the middle term using these numbers**

$2x^2 + 2x + 3x + 3$

📌 **Step 4: Factor by grouping**

$2x(x + 1) + 3(x + 1)$

📌 **Step 5: Factor out the common binomial**

$(x + 1)(2x + 3)$

✅ **Final Answer:**

$(x + 1)(2x + 3)$

---

### **(D) Factoring Special Products**

There are three important patterns to memorize:

1. **Difference of Squares**

   $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$

   **Example:**

   $x^2 - 16 = (x - 4)(x + 4)$
2. **Perfect Square Trinomials**

   $a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = (a + b)^2$

   **Example:**

   $x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2$
3. **Sum & Difference of Cubes**

   $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$

   **Example:**

   $x^3 - 8 = (x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4)$

## **3. Real-World Applications of Factoring**

### **(A) Physics & Engineering ⚙️**

Engineers use factoring to **simplify equations in mechanics, circuit analysis, and motion calculations**.

### **(B) Business & Finance 💰**

Businesses use factoring to **analyze profit models and optimize expenses**.

### **(C) Computer Science & Cryptography 💻**

Factoring plays a role in **encryption and cybersecurity algorithms**.

## **4. Key Steps for Success 🎯**

✅ **Always look for the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) first**.  
✅ **For four-term polynomials, use grouping**.  
✅ **For trinomials, find two numbers that multiply to $ac$ and add to $b$**.  
✅ **Memorize special product formulas to speed up your work**.  
✅ **Check your answer by multiplying the factors back together!**

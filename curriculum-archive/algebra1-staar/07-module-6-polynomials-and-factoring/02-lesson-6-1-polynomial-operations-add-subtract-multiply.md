# **Lesson 6.1: Polynomial Operations**

## **Why Should You Care About Polynomial Operations?**

Imagine designing a roller coaster, modeling business profits, or even predicting population growth—all of these rely on **polynomials**! Mastering polynomial operations will help you manipulate and analyze complex mathematical expressions with ease.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **add, subtract, and multiply polynomials** while also identifying key characteristics like **degree** and **leading coefficient**.

---

## **1. Understanding Polynomials**

A **polynomial** is an algebraic expression made up of terms consisting of **variables**, **coefficients**, and **exponents** that are **whole numbers**.

### **(A) Identifying Parts of a Polynomial**

Consider the polynomial:

$3x^4 - 5x^2 + 2x - 7$

* **Terms:** $3x^4$, $-5x^2$, $2x$, $-7$
* **Coefficient:** The number in front of a variable (e.g., **3** in $3x^4$)
* **Exponent:** The power of the variable (e.g., **4** in $x^4$)
* **Constant:** A term without a variable (e.g., $-7$)
* **Degree of the Polynomial:** The highest exponent ($4$ in this case)
* **Leading Coefficient:** The coefficient of the highest-degree term (**3** in this case)

---

## **2. Adding and Subtracting Polynomials**

When adding or subtracting polynomials, **combine like terms** (terms with the same variable and exponent).

#### **Example 1: Adding Polynomials**

$(4x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 6) + (3x^3 - x^2 + 7x - 2)$

📌 **Step 1: Group Like Terms**

$(4x^3 + 3x^3) + (2x^2 - x^2) + (-5x + 7x) + (6 - 2)$

📌 **Step 2: Simplify**

$7x^3 + x^2 + 2x + 4$

✅ **Final Answer:** $7x^3 + x^2 + 2x + 4$

---

#### **Example 2: Subtracting Polynomials**

$(5x^4 - 3x^3 + 2x - 9) - (2x^4 + x^3 - 4x + 5)$

📌 **Step 1: Distribute the Negative**

$5x^4 - 3x^3 + 2x - 9 - 2x^4 - x^3 + 4x - 5$

📌 **Step 2: Group Like Terms**

$(5x^4 - 2x^4) + (-3x^3 - x^3) + (2x + 4x) + (-9 - 5)$

📌 **Step 3: Simplify**

$3x^4 - 4x^3 + 6x - 14$

✅ **Final Answer:** $3x^4 - 4x^3 + 6x - 14$

---

## **3. Multiplying Polynomials**

To multiply polynomials, use the **distributive property** (or FOIL method for binomials).

### **(A) Multiplying a Monomial by a Polynomial**

#### **Example 3:**

$3x^2(2x^3 - 4x + 5)$

📌 **Step 1: Distribute**

$(3x^2 \cdot 2x^3) + (3x^2 \cdot -4x) + (3x^2 \cdot 5)$

📌 **Step 2: Simplify**

$6x^5 - 12x^3 + 15x^2$

✅ **Final Answer:** $6x^5 - 12x^3 + 15x^2$

---

### **(B) Multiplying Binomials Using FOIL**

#### **Example 4:**

$(x + 3)(x - 5)$

📌 **Step 1: Apply FOIL (First, Outer, Inner, Last)**

* **First:** $x \cdot x = x^2$
* **Outer:** $x \cdot -5 = -5x$
* **Inner:** $3 \cdot x = 3x$
* **Last:** $3 \cdot -5 = -15$

📌 **Step 2: Combine Like Terms**

$x^2 - 5x + 3x - 15$ $x^2 - 2x - 15$

✅ **Final Answer:** $x^2 - 2x - 15$

---

### **(C) Multiplying Polynomials (Distributive Method)**

#### **Example 5:**

$(x^2 + 2x + 1)(x - 3)$

📌 **Step 1: Distribute Each Term**

$x^2(x - 3) + 2x(x - 3) + 1(x - 3)$

📌 **Step 2: Multiply**

$x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x^2 - 6x + x - 3$

📌 **Step 3: Combine Like Terms**

$x^3 - x^2 - 5x - 3$

✅ **Final Answer:** $x^3 - x^2 - 5x - 3$

---

## **4. Key Concepts: Degrees and Leading Coefficients**

### **(A) Degree of a Polynomial**

The **degree** is the highest exponent of the variable.

| Polynomial | Highest Exponent | Degree |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $5x^4 - 2x^2 + 7$ | 4 | 4th degree |
| $3x^3 + 2x + 6$ | 3 | 3rd degree |
| $x^2 - 4x + 9$ | 2 | 2nd degree (quadratic) |

### **(B) Leading Coefficient**

The **leading coefficient** is the coefficient of the highest-degree term.

#### **Example 6:**

$7x^5 - 4x^3 + 9x - 2$

* **Degree:** 5
* **Leading Coefficient:** **7**

## **Lesson 5.3: Applications of Systems of Equations**

### **Why Should You Care About Systems of Equations?**

Ever wanted to compare two phone plans to see which one is cheaper in the long run? Or figure out when two people traveling at different speeds will meet? Systems of equations allow you to solve problems that involve **two or more things happening at once.**

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to write and solve systems of equations based on real-life situations—and understand exactly what your solution means in context.

---

## **By the End of This Lesson, You’ll Be Able To:**

* Write a system of equations from a word problem
* Solve it using substitution, elimination, or graphing
* Interpret what the solution means in the context of the situation
* Apply systems to financial, motion, and comparison problems

---

## **1. What Is a System of Equations?**

A **system of equations** is a set of two or more equations that you solve **together**.

Each equation represents a different condition or part of a real-world situation. The **solution** is the point where both equations are true at the same time.

✅ One solution = one point of intersection (they meet!)  
❌ No solution = lines never cross (parallel)  
♾ Infinite solutions = same line (they overlap)

---

## **2. Real-World Situations that Use Systems**

Systems of equations are used to solve problems like:

* Comparing two pricing plans or service options
* Figuring out how long until two people traveling at different speeds meet
* Determining the number of items sold given a total cost and total quantity
* Solving mixture or investment problems

---

## **3. Example 1: Comparing Plans**

**Situation:**  
Plan A charges $20 a month plus $2 per GB of data.  
Plan B charges $10 a month plus $3 per GB.  
At what point do the plans cost the same?

**Step 1: Define variables**  
Let $x =$ number of GBs  
Let $y =$ total cost

**Step 2: Write a system**

* Plan A: $y = 20 + 2x$
* Plan B: $y = 10 + 3x$

**Step 3: Solve the system** (use substitution or set equations equal)

$20 + 2x = 10 + 3x$ $10 = x$

**Step 4: Interpret the answer**  
At **10 GB**, both plans cost the same. ✅

---

## **4. Example 2: Motion Problem**

**Situation:**  
You and your friend start walking from different places toward each other.

* You walk at 3 mph.
* Your friend walks at 2 mph.
* You’re 10 miles apart.  
  How long until you meet?

**Step 1: Let $x$ be the time in hours**

* Your distance: $3x$
* Friend’s distance: $2x$
* Together: $3x + 2x = 10$

**Step 2: Write the equation**

$5x = 10$

**Step 3: Solve**

$x = 2$

✅ You’ll meet in **2 hours**.

---

## **5. Helpful Strategies for Success**

✅ **Organize your variables.** Let $x$ and $y$ represent meaningful quantities.  
✅ **Turn words into math.** Identify rates, totals, and quantities in the problem.  
✅ **Label your final answer.** Include units (miles, hours, dollars, etc.)  
✅ **Check your solution** by plugging it back into both equations.  
✅ **Practice with real examples**—it gets easier over time!

## **6. Real-Life Applications You Might Encounter**

### 💸 Budgeting or Pricing Plans

Use systems to compare two subscription services and find out when they cost the same.

### 🚗 Travel & Motion

Two people traveling at different speeds—when do they meet? How far apart do they start?

### 🛒 Sales & Profits

A business sells 2 items. You know total items and total profit. Use a system to find how many of each were sold.

### 🧪 Mixture Problems

How much of each solution do you need to mix for a specific concentration? (This comes up in chemistry and cooking!)

# **Lesson 5.5: Graphing Systems of Inequalities**

## **Why Should You Care About Graphing Linear Inequalities?**

Ever heard of "at least" or "no more than" in real life? Whether you’re budgeting money, setting a speed limit, or tracking how much time you need to study, inequalities help define **ranges** instead of exact values. **Graphing inequalities** lets you **see** those possible solutions instead of just listing them.

By the end of this lesson, you'll know how to **solve and graph linear inequalities** and **graph systems of linear inequalities to find solution regions**.

---

## **1. Solving and Graphing Linear Inequalities**

### **(A) What is a Linear Inequality?**

A **linear inequality** is similar to a linear equation but uses inequality symbols instead of an equal sign:

$>, \quad <, \quad \geq, \quad \leq$

💡 **Example 1:** Compare these two:  
🔹 **Equation:** $y = 2x + 3$ (exact solutions)  
🔹 **Inequality:** $y > 2x + 3$ (a range of solutions)

Instead of just a **line**, an inequality represents a **shaded region** where all possible solutions exist!

---

### **(B) Steps to Graph a Linear Inequality**

💡 **Example 2:** Graph $y \leq -\frac{1}{2}x + 4$.

🔹 **Step 1: Graph the boundary line**

* Rewrite the inequality as an equation: **$y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 4$**.
* **Identify the slope $m = -\frac{1}{2}$** and y-intercept **$b = 4$**.
* Plot the y-intercept **(0,4)**.
* Use the slope to plot another point: **Move down 1, right 2** to get another point.
* **Solid or dashed line?**
  + **Solid line** for **$\leq$ or $\geq$**.
  + **Dashed line** for **$<$ or $>$**.
  + Here, use a **solid line** (because $\leq$ means "or equal to").

🔹 **Step 2: Shade the solution region**

* Pick a test point **not on the line** (the origin **(0,0)** is usually easiest).
* Plug (0,0) into the inequality: $0 \leq -\frac{1}{2}(0) + 4$ $0 \leq 4 \quad \text{(True!)}$
* Since the inequality is **true**, **shade the region containing (0,0)**.
* If the test point made the inequality **false**, shade the opposite side.

📌 **Key Tip:** Always double-check shading with a test point!

## **2. Graphing Systems of Linear Inequalities**

### **(A) What is a System of Linear Inequalities?**

A **system of linear inequalities** means **two or more inequalities graphed together**. The **solution** is the **overlapping shaded region** where both inequalities are true.

💡 **Example 3:** Graph the system:  
1️⃣ $y > x - 2$  
2️⃣ $y \leq -\frac{1}{2}x + 3$

🔹 **Step 1: Graph the first inequality**

* **Rewrite as $y = x - 2$**.
* Slope = **1**, y-intercept = **-2**.
* Use a **dashed** line (since it’s $>$, not $\geq$).
* Pick a test point (0,0) → **$0 > -2$** (True), so shade **above** the line.

🔹 **Step 2: Graph the second inequality**

* **Rewrite as $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 3$**.
* Slope = **$-\frac{1}{2}$**, y-intercept = **3**.
* Use a **solid** line (since it’s $\leq$, meaning "or equal to").
* Pick a test point (0,0) → **$0 \leq 3$** (True), so shade **below** the line.

🔹 **Step 3: Identify the solution region**

* The **solution** is the region **where both shaded areas overlap**!

📌 **Key Tip:** If there is **no overlap**, there is **no solution**!

## **3. Real-World Applications of Graphing Inequalities**

Inequalities appear **everywhere** in real life!

💡 **Example 4: Budgeting**  
You have **$50** to spend on snacks and drinks. Snacks cost **$5** each, and drinks cost **$2** each.

🔹 Let **x = number of snacks**, **y = number of drinks**.  
🔹 The inequality is:

$5x + 2y \leq 50$

🔹 Graph it and find possible **combinations of snacks and drinks** that fit your budget!

---

## **4. Key Steps for Success 🎯**

✅ **Use a dashed line for $<$ or $>$, and a solid line for $\leq$ or $\geq$.**  
✅ **Always test a point to check shading!**  
✅ **For systems, the solution is where the shaded regions overlap.**  
✅ **Think of real-world scenarios like money, limits, and restrictions!**

Mastering **graphing inequalities and systems** helps you understand **real-world constraints**. Whether you're **budgeting, setting limits, or making predictions**, inequalities let you see **all possible solutions** instead of just one!

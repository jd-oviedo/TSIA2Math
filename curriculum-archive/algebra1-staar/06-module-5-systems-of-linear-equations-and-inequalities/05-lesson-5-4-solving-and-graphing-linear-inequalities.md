### **Lesson 5.4: Solving and Graphing Linear Inequalities**

#### **Why Should You Care About Inequalities?**

Imagine you’re at an amusement park, and there’s a ride that only allows people who are **at least** 48 inches tall. If you’re taller than that, you can ride. If not, you’re out of luck. That rule can be written as an **inequality**:

$h \geq 48$

(Inequalities use symbols like **≥ (greater than or equal to)** and **≤ (less than or equal to)** to show a range of possible values.)

Inequalities show up in everyday life all the time—speed limits, budgets, time restrictions, and more. Let’s break them down so you can **understand, solve, and graph them like a pro!**

---

### **1. Writing, Solving, and Graphing One-Variable Inequalities**

An **inequality** is like an equation, but instead of saying two things are equal, it shows that one side is greater or less than the other.

#### **Inequality Symbols You Must Know:**

* **>** (greater than) → "More than"
* **<** (less than) → "Less than"
* **≥** (greater than or equal to) → "At least"
* **≤** (less than or equal to) → "At most"

#### **Solving Inequalities: The Basics**

Solving inequalities is just like solving equations—**with one BIG difference.** When you **multiply or divide by a negative number**, you **must flip the inequality sign**.

🔹 **Example 1: Solving a Simple Inequality**  
Solve for **x** in the inequality:

$3x - 5 < 10$

**Step 1:** Add 5 to both sides

$3x < 15$

**Step 2:** Divide by 3

$x < 5$

This means **x can be any number less than 5** (like 4, 2, 0, or even -100).

---

#### **Graphing One-Variable Inequalities**

To graph **x < 5**, we:  
✅ Draw a number line  
✅ Place an **open circle** at 5 (because it does NOT include 5)  
✅ Shade everything **to the left** of 5 (since x is **less than** 5)

🔹 **If the inequality is "greater than or equal to" (≥) or "less than or equal to" (≤), use a closed circle!**

---

### **2. Compound Inequalities: Double the Fun!**

A **compound inequality** combines two inequalities into one statement.

#### **Types of Compound Inequalities**

1️⃣ **AND (Intersection: Must satisfy BOTH conditions at the same time)**

* Example:

$2 \leq x < 7$

This means **x must be between 2 and 7** (including 2, but NOT 7).

✅ On a number line, you would **shade between** 2 and 7, using a **closed circle on 2** and an **open circle on 7**.

2️⃣ **OR (Union: Can satisfy EITHER condition, not necessarily both)**

* Example:

$x < -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x \geq 5$

This means **x can be less than -3 OR greater than or equal to 5**.

✅ On a number line, you shade in **two opposite directions**—one section going left from -3 (open circle), and one going right from 5 (closed circle).

---

### **3. Real-World Applications of Inequalities**

Inequalities are super useful in real life. Here are some examples:

✅ **Budgeting:** You have $40 to spend at the mall. If **x** is the amount you spend, then:

$x \leq 40$

You can spend **up to** $40, but not more.

✅ **Speed Limits:** If the speed limit is 65 mph, then:

$s \leq 65$

You can go **65 mph or slower**, but not faster (unless you want a ticket!).

✅ **School Requirements:** To be on the honor roll, you need a GPA of **at least** 3.5:

$g \geq 3.5$

This means 3.5 is the minimum, but you can have anything higher.

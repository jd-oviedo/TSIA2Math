# **Lesson 5.2: Solving Systems of Equations Graphically**

## **Why Should You Care About Systems of Equations?**

Imagine you and a friend are saving money for a big purchase, but you both start with different amounts and save at different rates. At some point, your savings will be equal. But when? **That’s exactly what solving systems of equations helps you figure out!**

By the end of this lesson, you’ll know how to **graph systems of equations**, **identify their solutions**, and **apply these concepts to real-world situations** like comparing phone plans, predicting business profits, or solving motion problems.

---

## **1. What Is a System of Equations?**

A **system of equations** is when you have **two or more equations** with the same variables. The **solution** is where both equations are **true at the same time**—which means their graphs **intersect** at a point.

### **Example of a System of Equations:**

$y = 2x + 3$ $y = -x + 6$

Graphing both lines will show where they meet. That intersection is the solution!

---

## **2. Types of Solutions for Systems of Equations**

### **(A) One Solution (Intersecting Lines) 📍**

* If two lines **intersect at one point**, the system has **one unique solution**.
* This means there is exactly **one set of values** that satisfies both equations.

💡 **Example 1:**  
Graph the system:

$y = 2x + 1$ $y = -x + 4$

These lines cross at **(1,3)**, so the solution is **$x = 1, y = 3$**.

📌 **Key Tip:** The solution to the system is **always** written as an **ordered pair (x, y)**.

---

### **(B) No Solution (Parallel Lines) 🚫**

* If two lines **never intersect**, they are **parallel** and have **no solution**.
* This happens when the equations have the **same slope but different y-intercepts**.

💡 **Example 2:**

$y = 2x + 5$ $y = 2x - 3$

Both have a slope of **2**, but different y-intercepts, so they **never cross** → **No Solution**.

📌 **Key Tip:** **Parallel lines always have the same slope but different y-values.**

---

### **(C) Infinite Solutions (Same Line) 🔄**

* If two equations **represent the same line**, they overlap completely and have **infinitely many solutions**.
* This means **any point** on the line is a solution.

💡 **Example 3:**

$y = 3x - 2$ $6x - 2y = 4$

If you simplify the second equation, you get **the same equation** as the first one! That means the system has **infinite solutions**.

📌 **Key Tip:** If two equations are **equivalent**, their graphs **coincide**, meaning they are the same line.

---

## **3. Solving Systems by Graphing**

To **solve a system of equations graphically**, follow these steps:

✅ **Step 1:** Convert both equations to **slope-intercept form** ($y = mx + b$).  
✅ **Step 2:** Graph each equation carefully.  
✅ **Step 3:** Identify the intersection point (if any).  
✅ **Step 4:** Write the solution as an **ordered pair (x, y)**.

---

## **4. Applications of Systems of Equations**

Solving systems of equations isn’t just a classroom exercise—it helps in **real life** too!

### **(A) Comparing Phone Plans 📱**

Company A charges $10 per month plus $0.05 per text.  
Company B charges **$0.10 per text with no monthly fee**.

Let $x$ be the **number of texts sent** and $y$ be the **total cost**:

🔹 **Company A:**

$y = 0.05x + 10$

🔹 **Company B:**

$y = 0.10x$

Graph both equations and **find the intersection**—that’s when both plans cost the same. If you text less than that, one plan is better. If you text more, the other plan is better.

---

### **(B) Business and Profits 💰**

A bakery sells cakes. Their **cost** equation is:

$y = 5x + 100$

where $x$ is the number of cakes made, and $y$ is total cost.

They **sell** cakes for $10 each, so their **revenue** equation is:

$y = 10x$

🔹 The point where **cost = revenue** is the **break-even point** (where they start making a profit).

Graphing these lines tells you exactly **how many cakes they must sell to cover their costs!**

---

## **5. Key Steps for Success 🎯**

✅ **Graph carefully—double-check your slopes and intercepts.**  
✅ **Look for intersection points—write solutions as (x, y).**  
✅ **Check for parallel lines (no solution) and overlapping lines (infinite solutions).**  
✅ **Use graphing calculators or Desmos for quick accuracy.**

---

Graphing systems of equations is **one of the most powerful tools in Algebra** because it helps us visualize solutions and apply them to real-world problems.

# **Lesson 5.1: Systems of Linear Equations**

## **Why Do We Need Systems of Equations?**

Imagine you and your friend are saving money for a concert. You save a fixed amount each week, but your friend saves at a different rate. **At what point will you both have the same amount saved?** That’s a system of equations in action!

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **solve systems of equations algebraically using substitution and elimination** and **apply them to real-world scenarios.**

---

## **1. What Is a System of Linear Equations?**

A **system of equations** is when you have **two or more equations** with the same variables. The goal is to find the **point (x, y) that makes both equations true**—this is called the **solution to the system**.

✅ **One Solution:** The lines intersect at one point.  
✅ **No Solution:** The lines are parallel (they never intersect).  
✅ **Infinite Solutions:** The lines are the same (they overlap completely).

---

## **2. Solving Systems Algebraically**

We have two powerful algebraic methods for solving systems: **substitution** and **elimination**.

### **Method 1: Substitution**

This method is great when one equation is already solved for a variable (like $y = \dots$ or $x = \dots$).

**Example:**

$y = 2x + 3$ $3x - y = 5$

✅ **Step 1: Substitute** $y = 2x + 3$ into the second equation:

$3x - (2x + 3) = 5$

✅ **Step 2: Solve for $x$**

$3x - 2x - 3 = 5$ $x - 3 = 5$ $x = 8$

✅ **Step 3: Plug $x = 8$ back into $y = 2x + 3$:**

$y = 2(8) + 3 = 16 + 3 = 19$

✅ **Solution:** $(8, 19)$

📌 **Key Tip:** Use substitution when one equation is already solved for $x$ or $y$. It keeps things simple!

---

### **Method 2: Elimination**

This method is great when both equations are in **standard form** ($Ax + By = C$) and you want to eliminate a variable by adding or subtracting.

**Example:**

$2x + 3y = 12$ $4x - 3y = 6$

✅ **Step 1: Add the equations to eliminate $y$:**

$(2x + 3y) + (4x - 3y) = 12 + 6$ $6x = 18$

✅ **Step 2: Solve for $x$**

$x = 3$

✅ **Step 3: Plug $x = 3$ into one of the original equations:**

$2(3) + 3y = 12$ $6 + 3y = 12$ $3y = 6$ $y = 2$

✅ **Solution:** $(3,2)$

📌 **Key Tip:** Use elimination when both equations are in standard form, and look for variables that can easily be eliminated. If necessary, **multiply one or both equations** to create matching coefficients.

## **3. Applying Systems of Equations in Real-World Problems**

### **(A) Comparing Cell Phone Plans 📱**

Imagine two phone companies offer different pricing plans:

* **Plan A:** $30 per month + $0.05 per text
* **Plan B:** $20 per month + $0.10 per text

How many texts would you need to send for both plans to cost the same?

Let $x$ be the number of texts.

$30 + 0.05x = 20 + 0.10x$

Solving for $x$, you’d find how many texts it takes for both plans to be equal in cost. This helps you choose the best plan!

---

### **(B) Ticket Sales 🎟️**

A concert venue sells two types of tickets:

* **VIP tickets** cost $50
* **General admission** costs $20

The venue made **$5,000 in total sales** and sold **200 tickets**. How many of each type were sold?

Let $x$ be VIP tickets and $y$ be general admission tickets.

$x + y = 200$ $50x + 20y = 5000$

Using substitution or elimination, you can find how many of each type were sold!

## **4. Key Steps for Success 🎯**

✅ **Decide which method to use** (Substitution works well when a variable is already isolated, while elimination works well when both equations are in standard form).  
✅ **Check your solution** by plugging it back into both equations.  
✅ **Apply systems to real-world problems** by setting up equations based on the given information.

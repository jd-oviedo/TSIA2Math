# **Lesson 4.5: Graphing Linear Functions and Key Features**

## **Why Should You Care About Graphing Linear Functions?**

Imagine trying to track your progress in a game, your savings over time, or even the speed of a car. **Graphing linear functions** helps you see patterns, make predictions, and understand relationships between numbers visually. When you **graph a line**, you’re essentially creating a **map** that tells a story about how two things are related!

By the end of this lesson, you’ll know how to **graph linear functions, identify intercepts and key points, and interpret slope visually**.

---

## **1. Identifying Intercepts, Slope, and Key Points of Linear Graphs**

### **(A) What is a Linear Function?**

A **linear function** is an equation that forms a **straight line** when graphed. The general form of a linear equation is:

$y = mx + b$

where:

* **$m$** is the slope (rate of change)
* **$b$** is the y-intercept (where the graph crosses the y-axis)

**Example:** The equation $y = 2x + 3$ represents a linear function. The slope is **2**, and the y-intercept is **3**.

---

### **(B) Identifying Intercepts**

**1️⃣ The Y-Intercept ($b$)**  
The **y-intercept** is where the line **crosses the y-axis**. To find it, set $x = 0$ in the equation and solve for $y$.

💡 **Example 1:** Find the y-intercept of **$y = -3x + 4$**.  
🔹 Set $x = 0$:

$y = -3(0) + 4 = 4$

✅ The y-intercept is **4**, meaning the graph crosses the y-axis at **(0,4)**.

---

**2️⃣ The X-Intercept**  
The **x-intercept** is where the line **crosses the x-axis**. To find it, set $y = 0$ in the equation and solve for $x$.

💡 **Example 2:** Find the x-intercept of **$y = -3x + 4$**.  
🔹 Set $y = 0$:

$0 = -3x + 4$ $3x = 4$ $x = \frac{4}{3}$

✅ The x-intercept is **$\left( \frac{4}{3}, 0 \right)$**, meaning the graph crosses the x-axis at **$x = \frac{4}{3}$**.

📌 **Key Tip:** The **y-intercept is always where $x = 0$, and the x-intercept is always where $y = 0$**.

---

### **(C) Understanding Slope on a Graph**

Remember, the **slope** ($m$) tells you how steep a line is and how it moves. On a graph, slope is found using **"rise over run"**:

$m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$

* **Rise**: The vertical change (up/down)
* **Run**: The horizontal change (left/right)

💡 **Example 3:** Find the slope of a line passing through **(2,3) and (6,7)**.

$m = \frac{7 - 3}{6 - 2} = \frac{4}{4} = 1$

✅ The slope is **1**, meaning the line increases **1 unit up for every 1 unit right**.

📌 **Key Tip:** The steeper the slope, the more rapid the change!

---

### **(D) Graphing Linear Functions Step by Step**

💡 **Example 4:** Graph the equation **$y = \frac{1}{2}x - 3$**

🔹 **Step 1: Identify the y-intercept**

* $b = -3$, so plot **(0, -3)** on the graph.

🔹 **Step 2: Use the slope $m = \frac{1}{2}$**

* **Rise = 1** (move **up** 1 unit)
* **Run = 2** (move **right** 2 units)
* From (0, -3), move **up 1, right 2** → Plot the point **(2, -2)**

🔹 **Step 3: Draw the line**

* Connect the points with a straight line extending in both directions.

📌 **Key Tip:** Always **plot at least two points** before drawing the line!

---

## **2. Key Features of a Linear Graph**

When analyzing a linear graph, focus on these features:

✔️ **Slope ($m$)** → Shows how the line moves  
✔️ **Y-Intercept ($b$)** → Where the line crosses the y-axis  
✔️ **X-Intercept** → Where the line crosses the x-axis  
✔️ **Key Points** → Any important points on the graph

💡 **Example 5: Real-World Application**  
A cell phone company charges a **$20 monthly fee** plus **$5 per gigabyte of data used**. This can be modeled as:

$y = 5x + 20$

* **Slope $m = 5$** → You pay **$5 per GB**
* **Y-Intercept $b = 20$** → You pay **$20 even with 0 GB**
* **X-Intercept** → Solving $0 = 5x + 20$, we find $x = -4$, which doesn’t make sense (negative data usage isn’t possible)

📌 **Key Takeaway:** **Slope tells you the rate of change, and intercepts help you understand starting points and limits!**

---

## **3. Key Steps for Success 🎯**

✅ **Always identify intercepts first** (where the graph crosses the axes).  
✅ **Use slope to move from one point to another** on the graph.  
✅ **Graph at least two points before drawing the line.**  
✅ **Think about real-world meaning**—graphs tell stories!

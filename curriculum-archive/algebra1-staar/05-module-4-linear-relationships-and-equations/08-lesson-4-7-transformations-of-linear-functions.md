# **Lesson 4.6: Transformations of Linear Functions**

## **Why Should You Care About Transformations of Linear Functions?**

Imagine taking a basic line and **shifting it up, down, left, or right**—or even **flipping it upside down**! That’s exactly what transformations do. They help us adjust linear equations to fit real-world situations, like predicting trends, adjusting costs, or modeling motion.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **identify and apply transformations** such as **vertical and horizontal shifts, reflections, and dilations** to linear functions.

---

## **1. Understanding Function Transformations**

A **transformation** changes the appearance of a graph without altering its fundamental structure. We apply transformations to the **parent function** of a linear equation:

$f(x) = x$

Each transformation modifies the function in a different way.

---

## **2. Vertical and Horizontal Shifts**

### **(A) Vertical Shifts (Up or Down) 📈📉**

A **vertical shift** moves the graph **up or down** but keeps its slope the same.

🔹 **Equation Format:**

$f(x) = x + k$

* If **$k$ is positive**, the graph moves **up**.
* If **$k$ is negative**, the graph moves **down**.

💡 **Example 1:** Compare the graphs of:

* $f(x) = x$
* $g(x) = x + 3$ (Shift **up** 3)
* $h(x) = x - 4$ (Shift **down** 4)

📌 **Key Tip:** Only the **y-values** change in a vertical shift!

---

### **(B) Horizontal Shifts (Left or Right) ⬅️➡️**

A **horizontal shift** moves the graph **left or right**.

🔹 **Equation Format:**

$f(x) = (x - h)$

* If **$h$ is positive**, the graph moves **right**.
* If **$h$ is negative**, the graph moves **left**.

💡 **Example 2:** Compare the graphs of:

* $f(x) = x$
* $g(x) = (x - 2)$ (Shift **right** 2)
* $h(x) = (x + 5)$ (Shift **left** 5)

📌 **Key Tip:** Horizontal shifts affect the **x-values**, and the sign inside the parentheses is **opposite** of the direction!

---

## **3. Reflections (Flipping a Graph)**

A **reflection** flips the graph over an axis, changing its direction.

### **(A) Reflection Across the X-Axis 🔄**

🔹 **Equation Format:**

$f(x) = -x$

* The graph flips **upside down** (negative slope).
* Positive y-values become negative, and negative y-values become positive.

💡 **Example 3:**

* $f(x) = x$
* $g(x) = -x$ (**Reflected over the x-axis**)

📌 **Key Tip:** Reflection over the **x-axis** changes the **sign of the slope**!

---

## **4. Dilations (Stretching or Compressing a Graph)**

A **dilation** changes the **steepness** of a graph by multiplying the function by a constant.

### **(A) Vertical Stretch and Compression**

🔹 **Equation Format:**

$f(x) = a x$

* If **$|a| > 1$**, the graph **stretches** (gets steeper).
* If **$0 < |a| < 1$**, the graph **compresses** (flattens).

💡 **Example 4:** Compare the graphs of:

* $f(x) = x$ (Normal slope)
* $g(x) = 2x$ (Steeper)
* $h(x) = \frac{1}{2}x$ (Flatter)

📌 **Key Tip:** Multiplying by a **larger** number makes the slope **steeper**, while multiplying by a **smaller** number makes it **flatter**.

---

## **5. Combining Transformations**

Sometimes, multiple transformations happen at once!

💡 **Example 5:** What happens to $f(x) = -2(x + 3) - 4$?  
🔹 **Step 1:** $(x + 3)$ → **Shift left** 3  
🔹 **Step 2:** $-2x$ → **Reflect over the x-axis and stretch** (steeper slope)  
🔹 **Step 3:** $-4$ → **Shift down** 4

Graphing all transformations step-by-step makes them easier to understand!

---

## **6. Real-World Applications of Transformations**

**Transformations are everywhere!** Whether adjusting data, modeling real-world events, or predicting future trends, transformations make equations **flexible** and **useful**.

💡 **Example 6: Business Pricing Strategy**  
A store sells a product with a base price of $20. Due to demand, they **increase** the price by $5 every month. The function modeling the price is:

$f(x) = 20 + 5x$

🔹 **$+5x$** = Price increases over time (**vertical shift up**)  
🔹 **Graphing this** shows how pricing changes over months!

---

## **7. Key Steps for Success 🎯**

✅ **Recognize each transformation separately before combining them.**  
✅ **Watch out for horizontal shifts—the signs inside the parentheses are reversed!**  
✅ **Reflections flip the graph over an axis—don’t confuse them with shifts!**  
✅ **Vertical stretches make the graph steeper, while compressions make it flatter.**

---

## **Final Thoughts**

Transformations give us control over **how** a function behaves. Whether **shifting, flipping, or stretching a graph**, these tools help us **adjust equations to fit real-world situations**.

**Keep practicing, and soon, you’ll be able to transform any function like a pro! 🚀**

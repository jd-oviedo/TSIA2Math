# **Lesson 4.7: Parallel and Perpendicular Lines**

### **Why Should You Care About Parallel and Perpendicular Lines?**

Think about the roads in a city—some run parallel, never crossing, while others intersect at right angles. Understanding **parallel and perpendicular lines** is crucial in fields like engineering, design, and even video game development! In this lesson, you’ll learn to **identify their slopes** and **write their equations** like a pro.

---

## **1. Understanding Slopes of Parallel and Perpendicular Lines**

The **slope** of a line determines its **steepness** and **direction**. In general:

* A **positive slope** rises from left to right.
* A **negative slope** falls from left to right.
* A **zero slope** is a horizontal line.
* An **undefined slope** is a vertical line.

So how do slopes relate to parallel and perpendicular lines?

### **(A) Parallel Lines**

🔹 **Definition:** **Parallel lines never intersect** and always have the **same slope**.

🔹 **Slope Rule:** If two lines are parallel, their slopes are **equal**.

💡 **Example:**

* Line 1: $y = 2x + 3$
* Line 2: $y = 2x - 5$

Both have a slope of **$m = 2$**, so they are **parallel**!

📌 **Key Tip:** If two equations are in different forms, **rearrange them into slope-intercept form** ($y = mx + b$) to compare their slopes easily.

---

### **(B) Perpendicular Lines**

🔹 **Definition:** **Perpendicular lines intersect at a right angle (90°).**

🔹 **Slope Rule:** If two lines are perpendicular, their slopes are **opposite reciprocals**.

This means if one line has a slope of **$m$**, the perpendicular line’s slope is **$-\frac{1}{m}$**.

💡 **Example:**

* Line 1: $y = \frac{3}{4}x + 2$ → **Slope is $\frac{3}{4}$**
* Line 2: $y = -\frac{4}{3}x + 1$ → **Slope is $-\frac{4}{3}$**

Since **$-\frac{4}{3}$ is the opposite reciprocal of $\frac{3}{4}$**, the lines are **perpendicular**!

📌 **Key Tip:** The product of the slopes of two perpendicular lines is always **$-1$**.

$m\_1 \times m\_2 = -1$

If you multiply the slopes and don’t get **$-1$**, the lines **aren't perpendicular**.

---

## **2. Writing Equations for Parallel and Perpendicular Lines**

Now that you understand the slopes, let’s write **equations** for parallel and perpendicular lines.

### **(A) Writing an Equation for a Parallel Line**

🔹 **Steps:**

1. Find the given line’s **slope**.
2. Use the **same slope** for the new line.
3. Use the **point-slope formula** if a point is given: $y - y\_1 = m(x - x\_1)$

💡 **Example:** Write an equation of a line **parallel** to $y = -3x + 4$ passing through $(2,5)$.

1. The given line’s **slope is $-3$**.
2. A parallel line must have **the same slope** $m = -3$.
3. Using the **point-slope formula**: $y - 5 = -3(x - 2)$
4. Distribute: $y - 5 = -3x + 6$
5. Solve for $y$: $y = -3x + 11$

✅ The equation of the parallel line is **$y = -3x + 11$**.

---

### **(B) Writing an Equation for a Perpendicular Line**

🔹 **Steps:**

1. Find the given line’s **slope**.
2. Use the **opposite reciprocal** for the new line’s slope.
3. Use the **point-slope formula** with a given point.

💡 **Example:** Write an equation of a line **perpendicular** to $y = \frac{1}{2}x - 7$ passing through $(4,1)$.

1. The given line’s **slope is $\frac{1}{2}$**.
2. The perpendicular slope is the **opposite reciprocal** of $\frac{1}{2}$, which is **$-2$**.
3. Use the **point-slope formula**: $y - 1 = -2(x - 4)$
4. Distribute: $y - 1 = -2x + 8$
5. Solve for $y$: $y = -2x + 9$

✅ The equation of the perpendicular line is **$y = -2x + 9$**.

---

## **3. Helpful Hints for Success** 🎯

✅ **Parallel lines = same slope.** No need to overthink it! If the slope is the same, the lines are parallel.

✅ **Perpendicular lines = opposite reciprocals.** Flip the fraction and switch the sign.

✅ **Point-slope form is your best friend.** It’s the quickest way to write an equation when you know a point and a slope.

✅ **Always simplify your equation.** Convert to slope-intercept form ($y = mx + b$) when possible for easy graphing.

By mastering **parallel and perpendicular lines**, you’re unlocking a key skill for solving geometric problems, designing structures, and interpreting real-world relationships. Keep practicing, and soon you’ll be identifying and writing equations like a pro! 🚀

Welcome to your first big step into Algebra 1! In this lesson, you'll learn how to solve different types of equations—starting with the basics and working your way up to more complex problems. Mastering this skill will help you in future math topics and real-life situations, like budgeting your money, calculating discounts, or even figuring out how long it will take to finish a task.

### **What is an Equation?**

An equation is like a balanced scale—what happens on one side must also happen on the other. The goal is to figure out the value of the variable (usually represented by a letter like *x*) that makes the equation true. Think of it like solving a mystery—your job is to uncover what number the variable is hiding!

---

## **1. Solving One-Step, Two-Step, and Multi-Step Equations**

### **One-Step Equations**

These are the simplest types of equations. You only need to do one operation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division) to isolate the variable.

**Example 1: Addition/Subtraction**  
Solve for *x*:

$x + 7 = 12$

To get *x* alone, apply the inverse operation of +7. Subtract 7 from both sides:

$x = 5$

**Example 2: Multiplication/Division**  
Solve for *y*:

$5y = 20$

Divide both sides by 5:

$y = 4$

✅ **Quick Tip:** Whatever you do to one side of the equation, always do the same to the other side!

---

### **Two-Step Equations**

These equations require two operations to solve. Think of it as peeling layers off an onion—work step by step until the variable is isolated.

**Example 3:**  
Solve for *x*:

$3x - 5 = 10$

1. Add 5 to both sides:

3x = 15

2. Divide by 3:

$x = 5$

✅ **Quick Tip:** Work in reverse order of operations—undo addition/subtraction first, then division/multiplication!

---

### **Multi-Step Equations**

These require more than two steps and may include distributing or combining like terms before solving.

**Example 4:**  
Solve for *x*:

$2(x - 3) = 8$

1. Distribute the 2: $2x - 6 = 8$

2. Add 6 to both sides: $2x = 14$

3. Divide by 2: $x = 7$

✅ **Quick Tip:** If parentheses are involved, always distribute first before solving!

---

## **2. Solving Equations with Variables on Both Sides**

Sometimes, variables show up on both sides of the equation. The trick is to move them all to one side before solving.

**Example 5:**

Solve for x:

5x + 3 = 2x + 12

1. Subtract 2x from both sides to move all x-terms to one side:

3x + 3 = 12

2. Subtract 3 from both sides:

3x = 9

3. Divide by 3:

**x = 3**

✅ **Quick Tip:** Always simplify both sides as much as possible before moving terms around!

---

## **3. Using Proportions and Percent Equations in Problem-Solving**

### **Solving Proportions**

A proportion is an equation that shows two ratios are equal. You can solve it using cross-multiplication.

**Example 6:**

$\frac{3}{4} = \frac{x}{8}$

Cross multiply:

3(8)=4(x)

$3(8) = 4(x)$ $24 = 4x$

Divide by 4:

$x = 6$

✅ **Quick Tip:** Proportions are useful for real-world problems, like adjusting a recipe or scaling a drawing!

---

### **Solving Percent Equations**

Percent problems are everywhere—shopping discounts, tips at restaurants, and taxes. The key equation to remember is:

$\text{Part} = \text{Percent} \times \text{Whole}$

**Example 7: Finding the Part**  
What is 20% of 50?

x=0.20×50

$x = 0.20 \times 50$ $x = 10$

So, 20% of 50 is 10.

**Example 8: Finding the Whole**  
If 15 is 25% of a number, what is the number?

$15 = 0.25 \times W$

Divide by 0.25:

$W = 60$

So, the whole is 60.

✅ **Quick Tip:** Convert percentages to decimals before multiplying! Move the decimal two places left (20% = 0.20).

By the end of this lesson, you should feel confident solving different types of equations and applying them to real-life scenarios. Keep practicing, and you’ll be ready for the next challenge! 🚀

# **Lesson 4.1: Direct Variation**

### **Why Should You Care About Direct Variation?**

Have you ever noticed how the **more hours you work, the more money you earn** (assuming a fixed hourly wage)? Or how the **faster you drive, the farther you travel in the same amount of time**? These are examples of **direct variation**, a fundamental concept in Algebra that shows a constant relationship between two variables.

Understanding direct variation will help you **recognize proportional relationships**, **solve real-world problems**, and **analyze data trends** in everything from physics to economics!

---

## **1. Identifying and Graphing Direct Variation Equations**

### **(A) What is Direct Variation?**

🔹 **Definition:** A relationship between two variables, $x$ and $y$, where **$y$ is directly proportional to $x$**.

🔹 **Equation Format:**

$y = kx$

where $k$ is the **constant of variation** (also called the **constant of proportionality**).

📌 **Key Characteristics:**  
✅ The graph of a direct variation equation is always a **straight line** that **passes through the origin (0,0)**.  
✅ The **slope of the line is $k$**, which represents the constant rate of change.

💡 **Example:** Suppose $y$ varies directly with $x$, and when $x = 5$, $y = 15$.

1. Start with the direct variation equation: $y = kx$
2. Plug in the given values: $15 = k(5)$
3. Solve for $k$: $k = \frac{15}{5} = 3$
4. The equation is: $y = 3x$

✅ This means that for every **1 unit increase in $x$, $y$ increases by 3**.

---

### **(B) Graphing Direct Variation Equations**

To **graph a direct variation equation** like $y = 3x$:

1. **Plot the origin** $(0,0)$ since all direct variation graphs pass through it.
2. **Use the constant of variation ($k$) as the slope**:
   * Since $k = 3$, move **up 3** and **right 1** to find another point.
3. **Connect the points with a straight line**.

📌 **Key Tip:**  
If $k$ is **positive**, the graph **rises** from left to right.  
If $k$ is **negative**, the graph **falls** from left to right.

---

## **2. Applying Direct Variation in Real-World Scenarios**

Direct variation appears **everywhere in real life!** Let’s look at some common applications.

### **(A) Speed and Distance**

🔹 The **distance traveled** ($d$) varies directly with the **time** ($t$) when moving at a constant speed.

$d = rt$

where $r$ is the **rate (speed)**.

💡 **Example:** If a car is moving at **60 miles per hour**, the equation is:

$d = 60t$

This means that **in 2 hours, the car will travel $60(2) = 120$ miles**.

---

### **(B) Work and Pay**

🔹 If you earn a fixed hourly wage, your **total earnings ($E$)** vary directly with the **hours worked ($h$)**.

$E = kh$

💡 **Example:** If you make **$12 per hour**, the equation is:

$E = 12h$

So if you work **8 hours**, you will earn **$12(8) = 96$ dollars**.

---

### **(C) Chemistry and Physics Applications**

* The **weight of an object on Earth** varies directly with its **mass**.
* The **force applied to an object** varies directly with **acceleration** (Newton’s Second Law: $F = ma$).
* The **amount of ingredients in a recipe** varies directly with the number of servings you want to make.

---

## **3. Helpful Hints for Success** 🎯

✅ **Direct variation equations always have the form $y = kx$.** If there’s an extra number added or subtracted (like $y = kx + 5$), it’s **not** direct variation!

✅ **Check if a relationship is direct variation by dividing $y$ by $x$.** If $\frac{y}{x}$ is always the same, then it follows direct variation.

✅ **Graphs of direct variation equations always pass through the origin.** If a graph doesn’t pass through $(0,0)$, it’s not direct variation.

✅ **The constant of variation $k$ is the slope of the line.** The bigger $|k|$, the steeper the line!

---

### **Final Thoughts**

Mastering direct variation helps you **recognize patterns, analyze data, and solve real-world problems efficiently**. Whether you’re calculating your paycheck, figuring out how long a trip will take, or scaling a recipe, **direct variation is everywhere!** Keep practicing, and soon you’ll be identifying and graphing these relationships like a pro. 🚀

This is your first step into a world where numbers, symbols, and logic come together to solve problems. In this lesson, you’ll explore the basics of Algebra, including variables, constants, and how to translate words into mathematical expressions. Let’s dive in!

---

### **What is Algebra?**

Algebra is like a puzzle where you use symbols (usually letters) to represent unknown numbers or values. Instead of just working with numbers, you’ll use these symbols to find solutions to problems.

Think of Algebra as a language that helps you describe patterns, solve real-world problems, and understand relationships between numbers. For example, if you’re trying to figure out how much money you’ll have after saving $10 each week, Algebra can help you express and solve that problem easily.

It’s not just about solving for $x$ or doing “hard math.” It’s a tool that helps you figure out things in life, like how much money you’ll save, how long a road trip will take, or even how to predict trends in social media. Algebra gives you the power to think logically, solve problems, and discover the hidden rules behind the world around you.

---

### **Why Do We Need Algebra?**

Think of Algebra like a universal language. It might not help you order a pizza, but it will help you:

* **Plan your budget**: Want to save for a new phone or game? Algebra can show you how long it’ll take if you save a certain amount each week.
* **Understand data**: Ever wonder how a TikTok or YouTube video goes viral? Algebra is behind the numbers that track those views, likes, and shares.
* **Predict outcomes**: Like solving mysteries? Algebra helps scientists, engineers, and even game developers predict what might happen next.

#### **Real-World Example**

Let’s say you want to buy a pair of sneakers that cost $100. You’re saving $10 a week. Algebra can help you figure out how many weeks it will take to save enough:

$\text{Total cost} = 100, \quad \text{Saving per week} = 10$

In Algebra, you’d write:

$10x = 100$

Solve for $x$, and you find out it’ll take **10 weeks**!

You use Algebra more often than you think. Here are a few examples of how it shows up in everyday life:

### **Algebra is Like Solving Puzzles**

Think of Algebra as a giant puzzle where each equation is a clue leading to the answer. You might see a problem like this:

$2x + 5 = 15$

Your job is to figure out what $x$ (the mystery number) is. It’s like cracking a code! You’ll subtract 5 from both sides and then divide by 2:

$2x = 10 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 5$

Congratulations! You just solved an equation.

> **Hint for Success**: Solving equations is like following a recipe—take it step by step, and you’ll always get the right answer.

### **Variables, Constants, and Algebraic Expressions**

In Algebra, we work with three key elements: **variables**, **constants**, and **algebraic expressions**. Let’s break each one down.

#### **Variables**

A **variable** is a symbol (usually a letter) that represents an unknown or changeable value.

* Examples: $x$, $y$, $n$, $t$

> **Hint**: Think of a variable as a "mystery number" that can change depending on the situation.

#### **Constants**

A **constant** is a fixed number that doesn’t change.

* Examples: $3$, $5$, $-7$, $12$

#### **Algebraic Expressions**

An **algebraic expression** combines variables, constants, and mathematical operations (like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division).

* Examples:
  + $2x + 5$ (two times a number plus five)
  + $3y - 7$ (three times a number minus seven)

> **Important Tip**: Algebraic expressions don’t have an equals sign. They’re like phrases in a sentence, not complete sentences.

### **Translating Verbal Expressions into Algebraic Expressions**

One of the most important skills in Algebra is learning how to translate words into math. You’ll often see word problems that require turning verbal expressions into algebraic expressions.

Here are some common phrases and how they translate:

| **Phrase** | **Algebraic Translation** |
| --- | --- |
| A number increased by 5 | $x + 5$ |
| Twice a number | $2x$ |
| A number decreased by 7 | $x - 7$ |
| The product of a number and 3 | $3x$ |
| A number divided by 4 | $\frac{x}{4}$ |
| The sum of a number and 8 | $x + 8$ |
| Five less than a number | $x - 5$ |
| The difference between 10 and a number | $10 - x$ |

#### **Example 1: Translating a Phrase**

**Phrase**: "Three more than twice a number."  
**Translation**:

1. Identify the key parts:
   * "Twice a number" means $2x$.
   * "Three more than" means add 3.
2. Write the expression: $2x + 3$

#### **Example 2: Writing Your Own**

**Phrase**: "Seven less than three times a number."  
**Translation**:

1. "Three times a number" is $3x$.
2. "Seven less than" means subtract 7.
3. Expression: 3x−7

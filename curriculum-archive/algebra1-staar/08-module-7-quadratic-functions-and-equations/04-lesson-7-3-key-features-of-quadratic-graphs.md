# **Lesson 7.3: Key Features of Quadratic Graphs**

## **Why Should You Care About Quadratic Graphs?**

Quadratic graphs (parabolas) appear everywhere in **physics, engineering, finance, and nature**—from predicting the motion of objects to designing bridges and optimizing profits.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **identify the vertex, axis of symmetry, and intercepts of quadratic functions and use them to sketch graphs.**

---

## **1. Understanding Quadratic Graphs (Parabolas)**

A **quadratic function** is written as:

$f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$

The graph of a quadratic function is a **parabola** that can open **upward ($a > 0$)** or **downward ($a < 0$)**.

Key features of a parabola:  
✅ **Vertex** – The highest or lowest point  
✅ **Axis of Symmetry** – A vertical line dividing the parabola into two equal halves  
✅ **X-Intercepts (Roots/Zeros)** – Points where the graph crosses the x-axis  
✅ **Y-Intercept** – The point where the graph crosses the y-axis

---

## **2. Identifying the Key Features**

### **(A) Vertex**

The **vertex** is the turning point of the parabola. It can be found using:

$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$

Once you find $x$, substitute it into the equation to find $y$.

#### **Example 1: Finding the Vertex**

Find the vertex of $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$.

📌 **Step 1: Use the vertex formula**

$x = -\frac{-6}{2(1)} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$

📌 **Step 2: Substitute $x = 3$ into the equation**

$f(3) = (3)^2 - 6(3) + 5 = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4$

✅ **Vertex:** $(3, -4)$

---

### **(B) Axis of Symmetry**

The **axis of symmetry** is the vertical line that passes through the vertex.

$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$

The equation of the axis of symmetry is always **$x = \text{vertex's } x$-coordinate**.

✅ **For Example 1: Axis of Symmetry:** $x = 3$

---

### **(C) X-Intercepts (Zeros or Roots)**

X-intercepts are points where the parabola crosses the x-axis, found by solving $f(x) = 0$.

#### **Example 2: Finding X-Intercepts**

Find the x-intercepts of $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$.

📌 **Step 1: Solve $f(x) = 0$**

$x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$

📌 **Step 2: Factor**

$(x - 5)(x - 1) = 0$

📌 **Step 3: Solve for $x$**

$x - 5 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 5$ $x - 1 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 1$

✅ **X-Intercepts:** $(5, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$

---

### **(D) Y-Intercept**

The **y-intercept** is where the graph crosses the y-axis ($x = 0$).

#### **Example 3: Finding the Y-Intercept**

For $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$, set $x = 0$:

$f(0) = (0)^2 - 6(0) + 5 = 5$

✅ **Y-Intercept:** $(0, 5)$

---

## **3. Graphing Quadratic Functions**

### **Step 1: Identify Key Features**

* **Vertex**: $(3, -4)$
* **Axis of Symmetry**: $x = 3$
* **X-Intercepts**: $(5,0)$, $(1,0)$
* **Y-Intercept**: $(0,5)$

### **Step 2: Plot the Points**

1. **Plot the vertex**
2. **Draw the axis of symmetry** (dashed vertical line)
3. **Plot the x-intercepts and y-intercept**
4. **Reflect the y-intercept across the axis**
5. **Sketch the U-shaped parabola**

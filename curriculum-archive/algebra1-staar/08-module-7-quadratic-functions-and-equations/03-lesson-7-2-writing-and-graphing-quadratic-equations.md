# **Lesson 7.2: Writing and Graphing Quadratic Equations**

## **Why Should You Care About Quadratic Equations?**

Quadratic equations help us **model motion, predict outcomes, and analyze patterns in nature, business, and engineering**.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **write quadratic equations in different forms, convert between forms, and graph quadratic equations using key features**.

---

## **1. Understanding the Three Forms of Quadratic Equations**

A **quadratic equation** is an equation where the highest exponent of $x$ is 2. It is written as:

$f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$

There are **three main forms** of quadratic equations:

| **Form** | **Equation** | **Key Features** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard Form** | $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ | Y-intercept at $c$, use $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ for vertex |
| **Vertex Form** | $f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$ | Vertex at $(h, k)$, axis of symmetry at $x = h$ |
| **Factored Form** | $f(x) = a(x - r\_1)(x - r\_2)$ | X-intercepts at $r\_1, r\_2$, axis of symmetry at $x = \frac{r\_1 + r\_2}{2}$ |

Each form provides unique insights about the quadratic function.

---

## **2. Writing Quadratic Equations in Different Forms**

### **(A) Standard Form: $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$**

This form is useful for identifying the **y-intercept** and finding the **vertex using the formula**:

$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$

#### **Example 1: Writing an Equation in Standard Form**

Write the quadratic equation of a parabola with a vertex at $(2, -3)$ and passing through $(0,1)$.

📌 **Step 1: Start with vertex form**

$f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$ $f(x) = a(x - 2)^2 - 3$

📌 **Step 2: Substitute point (0,1) to solve for $a$**

$1 = a(0 - 2)^2 - 3$ $1 = a(4) - 3$ $4a = 4 \Rightarrow a = 1$

📌 **Step 3: Expand to standard form**

$f(x) = (x - 2)^2 - 3$ $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 4 - 3$ $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 1$

✅ **Equation in standard form:** $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 1$

---

### **(B) Vertex Form: $f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$**

This form is useful for **identifying the vertex** and understanding transformations of the graph.

#### **Example 2: Converting Standard Form to Vertex Form**

Convert $f(x) = x^2 + 6x + 8$ into vertex form.

📌 **Step 1: Complete the square**

$f(x) = x^2 + 6x + 8$ $f(x) = (x^2 + 6x) + 8$

Take half of **$b = 6$**, square it, and add/subtract inside the parentheses:

$f(x) = (x^2 + 6x + 9 - 9) + 8$ $f(x) = (x + 3)^2 - 9 + 8$ $f(x) = (x + 3)^2 - 1$

✅ **Vertex form:** $f(x) = (x + 3)^2 - 1$  
✅ **Vertex:** $(-3, -1)$

---

### **(C) Factored Form: $f(x) = a(x - r\_1)(x - r\_2)$**

This form is useful for finding the **x-intercepts (roots/zeros)**.

#### **Example 3: Converting Standard Form to Factored Form**

Factor $f(x) = x^2 - 5x + 6$.

📌 **Step 1: Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to -5**  
The numbers are **-2 and -3**.

📌 **Step 2: Rewrite in factored form**

$f(x) = (x - 2)(x - 3)$

✅ **Factored form:** $f(x) = (x - 2)(x - 3)$  
✅ **X-intercepts:** $x = 2, x = 3$

---

## **3. Graphing Quadratic Equations**

### **Step 1: Identify Key Features**

* **Vertex** (from vertex form or standard form calculation)
* **Axis of symmetry**: $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$
* **Intercepts**:
  + **Y-intercept** ($c$ in standard form)
  + **X-intercepts** (from factored form)

### **Step 2: Plot the Points**

1. **Plot the vertex**
2. **Draw the axis of symmetry** (dashed vertical line)
3. **Plot intercepts and additional points**
4. **Sketch the parabola** (U-shape, symmetric about the axis)

#### **Example 4: Graphing a Quadratic Function**

Graph $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3$.

📌 **Step 1: Identify key features**

* **Vertex:**

  $x = -\frac{-4}{2(1)} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$ $f(2) = (2)^2 - 4(2) + 3 = 4 - 8 + 3 = -1$

  ✅ **Vertex:** $(2, -1)$
* **Y-intercept:** $c = 3$ → **Point: $(0,3)$**
* **X-intercepts:** Factor $f(x) = (x - 3)(x - 1)$, so **roots are $x = 1, 3$**

📌 **Step 2: Plot vertex, axis, and intercepts**

* Draw the **axis of symmetry** at $x = 2$
* Plot **(2, -1), (0,3), (1,0), and (3,0)**
* Sketch a **U-shaped curve**

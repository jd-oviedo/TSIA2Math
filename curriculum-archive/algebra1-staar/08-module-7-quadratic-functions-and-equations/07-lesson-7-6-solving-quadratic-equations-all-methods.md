# **Lesson 7.6: Solving Quadratic Equations**

## **Why Should You Care About Solving Quadratics?**

Quadratic equations appear **everywhere**—from physics to finance to engineering. **Understanding how to solve them** helps you analyze motion, optimize business decisions, and model real-world situations.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **solve quadratic equations using factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula.**

---

## **1. What Is a Quadratic Equation?**

A quadratic equation is any equation that can be written in the standard form:

$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$

where:

* $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants
* $a \neq 0$ (if $a = 0$, it's a linear equation, not quadratic)

---

## **2. Solving Quadratics by Factoring**

### **(A) Zero Product Property**

If $ab = 0$, then either $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.

### **(B) Steps to Solve by Factoring**

1. **Write the equation in standard form**: $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
2. **Factor** the quadratic expression.
3. **Set each factor equal to zero** and solve for $x$.

📌 **Example 1: Factoring a Simple Quadratic**

Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$.

**Step 1:** Factor the quadratic:

$(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$

**Step 2:** Set each factor equal to zero:

$x - 2 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x - 3 = 0$

**Step 3:** Solve for $x$:

$x = 2 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 3$

✅ **Solution:** $x = 2, 3$

---

## **3. Solving Quadratics by Completing the Square**

**When to Use:** When the quadratic does not factor easily.

### **(A) Steps to Complete the Square**

1. Move the constant ($c$) to the other side: $x^2 + bx = -c$
2. Find **$\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$** and add it to both sides.
3. Rewrite the left-hand side as a **perfect square trinomial**.
4. Take the **square root** of both sides.
5. Solve for $x$.

📌 **Example 2: Completing the Square**

Solve $x^2 + 6x - 7 = 0$ by completing the square.

**Step 1:** Move $-7$ to the other side:

$x^2 + 6x = 7$

**Step 2:** Find $\left(\frac{6}{2}\right)^2 = 3^2 = 9$.

**Step 3:** Add **9** to both sides:

$x^2 + 6x + 9 = 7 + 9$ $(x + 3)^2 = 16$

**Step 4:** Take the square root of both sides:

$x + 3 = \pm 4$

**Step 5:** Solve for $x$:

$x = -3 \pm 4$ $x = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -7$

✅ **Solution:** $x = 1, -7$

---

## **4. Solving Quadratics Using the Quadratic Formula**

**When to Use:** Always works—especially useful when factoring is difficult.

### **(A) Quadratic Formula:**

$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

where:

* $a, b, c$ come from $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
* $\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$ is called the **discriminant** and determines the number of solutions.

---

### **(B) Understanding the Discriminant**

$D = b^2 - 4ac$

* **If $D > 0$** → **2 real solutions**
* **If $D = 0$** → **1 real solution** (perfect square)
* **If $D < 0$** → **No real solutions** (imaginary numbers)

---

📌 **Example 3: Using the Quadratic Formula**

Solve $2x^2 - 3x - 2 = 0$ using the quadratic formula.

**Step 1:** Identify $a = 2$, $b = -3$, $c = -2$.

**Step 2:** Plug into the quadratic formula:

$x = \frac{-(-3) \pm \sqrt{(-3)^2 - 4(2)(-2)}}{2(2)}$ $x = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 16}}{4}$ $x = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{25}}{4}$ $x = \frac{3 \pm 5}{4}$

**Step 3:** Solve for $x$:

$x = \frac{3 + 5}{4} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$ $x = \frac{3 - 5}{4} = \frac{-2}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}$

✅ **Solution:** $x = 2, -\frac{1}{2}$

---

## **5. Summary of Methods**

| Method | Best For | Steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Factoring** | Simple quadratics | Factor → Set factors to 0 → Solve |
| **Completing the Square** | Non-factorable quadratics | Move $c$ → Add $\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$ → Square root both sides |
| **Quadratic Formula** | Always works | Plug into $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ |

## **6. Real-World Applications of Quadratic Equations**

🏀 **Sports:** Calculating projectile motion of a basketball shot  
🏗️ **Engineering:** Designing parabolic bridges and arches  
📈 **Business:** Finding maximum profit in revenue models

---

## **7. Key Steps for Success 🎯**

✅ Identify the **best method** for solving the quadratic equation.  
✅ Always check if the equation is in **standard form** before solving.  
✅ Use **factoring when possible**, otherwise try completing the square or the quadratic formula.  
✅ Double-check your answers by **substituting them back** into the original equation.

# **Lesson 7.4: Factors and Zeros**

## **Why Should You Care About Factors and Zeros?**

Understanding the relationship between factors and zeros of quadratic functions **helps in solving equations, graphing parabolas, and analyzing real-world scenarios like projectile motion and profit models.**

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **connect the factors of a quadratic function to its x-intercepts (zeros) and use this knowledge to solve equations and graph quadratic functions.**

---

## **1. Understanding the Connection Between Factors and Zeros**

A quadratic function can be written in **factored form**:

$f(x) = a(x - r\_1)(x - r\_2)$

where:

* $r\_1$ and $r\_2$ are the **x-intercepts (zeros/roots)**
* The function equals zero when **$x = r\_1$ or $x = r\_2$**

This means **solving a quadratic equation is the same as finding its x-intercepts on a graph.**

---

## **2. Finding Zeros by Factoring**

To find the zeros of a quadratic function, **factor it and set it equal to zero.**

### **Example 1: Finding Zeros from Factored Form**

Find the zeros of:

$f(x) = (x - 4)(x + 2)$

📌 **Step 1: Set the function equal to zero**

$(x - 4)(x + 2) = 0$

📌 **Step 2: Solve for $x$**

$x - 4 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 4$ $x + 2 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = -2$

✅ **Zeros (x-intercepts):** $(4, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$

These points are where the graph crosses the **x-axis**.

---

## **3. Factoring Quadratic Equations to Find Zeros**

If the quadratic is not factored, we first **factor it** before solving for zeros.

### **Example 2: Finding Zeros from Standard Form**

Find the zeros of:

$f(x) = x^2 - 5x + 6$

📌 **Step 1: Factor the quadratic expression**

$x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 3)(x - 2)$

📌 **Step 2: Set each factor equal to zero**

$x - 3 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 3$ $x - 2 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 2$

✅ **Zeros (x-intercepts):** $(3,0)$ and $(2,0)$

---

## **4. Connecting Zeros to the Graph of a Quadratic Function**

* The zeros (x-intercepts) **represent where the graph crosses the x-axis**.
* The **vertex is halfway between the zeros** (use the midpoint formula).
* The **axis of symmetry** is the vertical line passing through the vertex.

---

## **5. Special Cases in Factoring**

### **(A) Quadratics with a GCF**

Factor out the greatest common factor (GCF) first.

#### **Example 3: Factor First**

$f(x) = 2x^2 - 8x$

📌 **Step 1: Factor out the GCF**

$2x(x - 4) = 0$

📌 **Step 2: Solve for $x$**

$2x = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 0$ $x - 4 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 4$

✅ **Zeros:** $(0,0)$ and $(4,0)$

---

### **(B) Difference of Squares**

Some quadratics factor as a **difference of squares**:

$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$

#### **Example 4: Using Difference of Squares**

$f(x) = x^2 - 9$

📌 **Step 1: Factor using difference of squares**

$(x - 3)(x + 3) = 0$

📌 **Step 2: Solve for $x$**

$x - 3 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 3$ $x + 3 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = -3$

✅ **Zeros:** $(3,0)$ and $(-3,0)$

---

## **6. Real-World Applications of Zeros and Factors**

🚀 **Physics:** Calculating when an object hits the ground  
🏀 **Sports:** Modeling the path of a thrown ball  
💰 **Business:** Finding break-even points in profit models

# **Lesson 7.1: Understanding Quadratic Functions**

## **Why Should You Care About Quadratic Functions?**

Quadratic functions are everywhere! They help us **model projectile motion, business profits, engineering structures, and more**.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **recognize quadratic functions in different forms, understand their key characteristics, and interpret their real-world applications**.

---

## **1. What Are Quadratic Functions?**

A **quadratic function** is any function that can be written in the form:

$f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$

where:

* **$a$ is the leading coefficient** (determines the direction and shape of the parabola)
* **$b$ affects the position of the vertex**
* **$c$ is the y-intercept**

Quadratic functions always graph as **parabolas**, which are U-shaped curves.

---

## **2. Recognizing Quadratic Functions in Different Forms**

### **(A) Standard Form**

$f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$

* The **y-intercept** is at **$c$**
* The **axis of symmetry** is at $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$
* The **vertex** (turning point) can be found using **$\left(-\frac{b}{2a}, f\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)\right)$**

#### **Example 1: Identifying Key Features in Standard Form**

$f(x) = 2x^2 - 4x + 1$

📌 **Step 1: Find the axis of symmetry**

$x = -\frac{-4}{2(2)} = \frac{4}{4} = 1$

📌 **Step 2: Find the vertex by substituting $x = 1$ into the function**

$f(1) = 2(1)^2 - 4(1) + 1 = 2 - 4 + 1 = -1$

✅ **Vertex:** $(1, -1)$  
✅ **Axis of symmetry:** $x = 1$  
✅ **Y-intercept:** $c = 1$

---

### **(B) Vertex Form**

$f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$

* **Vertex:** $(h, k)$
* **Axis of symmetry:** $x = h$
* **$a$** controls how wide or narrow the parabola is

#### **Example 2: Identifying Key Features in Vertex Form**

$f(x) = 3(x - 2)^2 + 5$

✅ **Vertex:** $(2, 5)$  
✅ **Axis of symmetry:** $x = 2$  
✅ **Direction:** Since $a = 3$ is positive, the parabola opens **upward**.

---

### **(C) Factored Form (Intercept Form)**

$f(x) = a(x - r\_1)(x - r\_2)$

* **$r\_1$ and $r\_2$ are the x-intercepts** (also called **zeros or roots**)
* **Axis of symmetry:** $x = \frac{r\_1 + r\_2}{2}$
* **Vertex can be found by substituting $x$-value of the axis of symmetry into the function**

#### **Example 3: Identifying Key Features in Factored Form**

$f(x) = (x - 3)(x + 5)$

📌 **Step 1: Find the x-intercepts**  
Setting $f(x) = 0$:

$(x - 3)(x + 5) = 0$

So, **$x = 3$ and $x = -5$** are the **x-intercepts**.

📌 **Step 2: Find the axis of symmetry**

$x = \frac{3 + (-5)}{2} = \frac{-2}{2} = -1$

📌 **Step 3: Find the vertex by substituting $x = -1$ into the function**

$f(-1) = (-1 - 3)(-1 + 5) = (-4)(4) = -16$

✅ **Vertex:** $(-1, -16)$  
✅ **Axis of symmetry:** $x = -1$

---

## **3. Comparing the Three Forms**

| **Form** | **Equation** | **Key Features** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard Form** | $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ | Y-intercept at $c$, use $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ for vertex |
| **Vertex Form** | $f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$ | Vertex at $(h, k)$, axis of symmetry at $x = h$ |
| **Factored Form** | $f(x) = a(x - r\_1)(x - r\_2)$ | X-intercepts at $r\_1, r\_2$, axis of symmetry at $x = \frac{r\_1 + r\_2}{2}$ |

Each form is useful depending on the context:  
✔ **Standard form** is great for finding intercepts and general equation structure.  
✔ **Vertex form** makes it easy to identify the vertex and transformations.  
✔ **Factored form** is best for finding roots quickly.

## **4. Real-World Applications of Quadratic Functions**

📊 **Physics:** Modeling projectile motion (e.g., a ball being thrown)  
💰 **Business:** Finding maximum profit using revenue functions  
🏗️ **Engineering:** Designing parabolic bridges and roller coasters  
🌱 **Biology:** Modeling population growth and decay

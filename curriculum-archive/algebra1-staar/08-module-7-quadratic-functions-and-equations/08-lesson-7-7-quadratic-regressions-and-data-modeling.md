# **Lesson 7.7: Quadratic Regression and Data Modeling**

## **Why Should You Care About Quadratic Regression?**

Quadratic models help us **analyze real-world data** that follows a curved pattern—like tracking the height of a ball in motion, predicting revenue growth, or modeling population trends.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **use quadratic regression to analyze data and make predictions.**

---

## **1. What Is Quadratic Regression?**

Quadratic regression is a method used to fit a **quadratic function** to a set of data points.

A quadratic function has the form:

$y = ax^2 + bx + c$

where:

* $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants determined by the data.
* The **graph** is a **parabola** that can open **upward** ($a > 0$) or **downward** ($a < 0$).

---

## **2. When Do We Use Quadratic Regression?**

We use quadratic regression when data:  
✅ Forms a **curved pattern** (not a straight line)  
✅ Has a **maximum or minimum point**  
✅ Follows a **parabolic trend**

📌 **Examples of Quadratic Data Patterns:**  
🏀 **Sports:** Predicting the path of a basketball shot  
📈 **Economics:** Modeling profit or revenue based on sales  
🌍 **Science:** Estimating population growth over time

---

## **3. How to Perform Quadratic Regression**

### **(A) Collect Data and Plot a Scatter Plot**

1. **Gather real-world data** that appears to follow a curved trend.
2. **Plot the points on a scatter plot** to visualize the shape of the data.

### **(B) Use a Graphing Calculator or Software to Find the Quadratic Model**

Most graphing calculators and software (Desmos, Excel, TI-84) can calculate quadratic regression.

#### **Steps Using a TI-84 Calculator:**

1. Press **STAT**, then choose **EDIT** to enter the data points.
2. Enter the $x$-values in **L1** and $y$-values in **L2**.
3. Press **STAT**, move to **CALC**, and choose **QuadReg (Quadratic Regression)**.
4. The calculator will output the **best-fit quadratic equation**:

   $y = ax^2 + bx + c$

### **(C) Interpret the Quadratic Equation**

* **$a$** tells us whether the parabola opens **upward** ($a > 0$) or **downward** ($a < 0$).
* **The vertex (h, k)** represents the **minimum or maximum** value of the data.
* The **$R^2$ value** tells us how well the quadratic model fits the data (closer to 1 = better fit).

---

## **4. Example of Quadratic Regression**

📊 **Example 1: Predicting the Height of a Thrown Ball**

A ball is thrown into the air, and its height is recorded at different times:

| Time (sec) | Height (ft) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 5 |
| 1 | 25 |
| 2 | 35 |
| 3 | 34 |
| 4 | 22 |
| 5 | 0 |

### **Step 1: Plot the Data**

The data points show a **parabolic pattern**—the ball rises and then falls.

### **Step 2: Perform Quadratic Regression**

Using a calculator, we get:

$h(t) = -4t^2 + 16t + 5$

### **Step 3: Interpret the Equation**

* **$a = -4$:** The parabola opens **downward** (gravity pulls the ball back down).
* **Maximum height:** The vertex tells us when the ball reaches its highest point.
* **Predicting future values:** We can plug in different values of $t$ to estimate height.

✅ **Application:** This equation helps **athletes**, **engineers**, and **scientists** predict motion!

---

## **5. Using Quadratic Models for Predictions**

Once you have a quadratic equation, you can:  
✅ **Find maximum or minimum values** (use the vertex formula $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$).  
✅ **Predict future values** by plugging in $x$-values.  
✅ **Determine when a value will reach a certain height or position** by solving for $x$.

📌 **Example 2: Business Application**

A company finds that its **profit** ($P$) in dollars, based on the price of a product ($x$), follows this model:

$P(x) = -2x^2 + 40x - 150$

🔹 **Find the optimal price for maximum profit.**

$x = -\frac{40}{2(-2)} = 10$

✅ **Best price to maximize profit:** $10

🔹 **Find the maximum profit.**

$P(10) = -2(10)^2 + 40(10) - 150 = 50$

✅ **Maximum profit:** $50

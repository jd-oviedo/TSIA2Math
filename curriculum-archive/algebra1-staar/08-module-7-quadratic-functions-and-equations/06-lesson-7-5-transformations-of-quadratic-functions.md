# **Lesson 7.5: Transformations of Quadratic Functions**

## **Why Should You Care About Quadratic Transformations?**

Transformations **help you understand how quadratic graphs change** when modified, making it easier to **graph equations, analyze real-world scenarios, and predict outcomes.**

By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to **describe and apply vertical and horizontal shifts, reflections, and stretches to quadratic functions.**

---

## **1. Understanding Quadratic Function Transformations**

The **parent function** of a quadratic is:

$f(x) = x^2$

When transformations are applied, the function takes the form:

$f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$

where:

* $h$ shifts the graph **left or right** (horizontal shift)
* $k$ shifts the graph **up or down** (vertical shift)
* $a$ controls **stretching, shrinking, or reflecting** the graph

---

## **2. Vertical and Horizontal Shifts**

Shifts **move** the parabola without changing its shape.

### **(A) Vertical Shifts – Moving Up or Down**

If $k > 0$, the graph moves **up**.  
If $k < 0$, the graph moves **down**.

📌 **Example 1: Vertical Shift**  
Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = x^2 + 3$.

* $g(x) = x^2 + 3$ **shifts the graph up** 3 units.
* The vertex moves from $(0,0)$ to $(0,3)$.

---

### **(B) Horizontal Shifts – Moving Left or Right**

If $h > 0$, the graph moves **right**.  
If $h < 0$, the graph moves **left**.

📌 **Example 2: Horizontal Shift**  
Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = (x - 4)^2$.

* $g(x) = (x - 4)^2$ **shifts the graph right** 4 units.
* The vertex moves from $(0,0)$ to $(4,0)$.

⚠️ **Remember:** The shift direction is opposite to the sign inside the parentheses.

---

## **3. Reflections Over the X-Axis**

If $a$ is **negative**, the parabola **flips (reflects) over the x-axis**.

📌 **Example 3: Reflection**  
Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = -x^2$.

* $g(x) = -x^2$ **reflects the graph downward**.
* The parabola opens **downward** instead of upward.

🔎 **Real-World Connection:**  
Reflections help model **inverted parabolic motion**, like the path of water from a flipped fountain nozzle.

---

## **4. Vertical and Horizontal Stretches and Shrinks**

Stretches and shrinks **change the "width" of the parabola**.

### **(A) Vertical Stretches and Shrinks**

If $|a| > 1$, the graph **stretches** (gets narrower).  
If $0 < |a| < 1$, the graph **shrinks** (gets wider).

📌 **Example 4: Vertical Stretch**  
Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = 2x^2$.

* $g(x) = 2x^2$ **stretches** the graph, making it **narrower**.

📌 **Example 5: Vertical Shrink**  
Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^2$.

* $g(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^2$ **shrinks** the graph, making it **wider**.

---

### **(B) Horizontal Stretches and Shrinks**

If $|b| > 1$, the graph **shrinks horizontally**.  
If $0 < |b| < 1$, the graph **stretches horizontally**.

📌 **Example 6: Horizontal Shrink**  
Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = (2x)^2$.

* $g(x) = (2x)^2$ makes the parabola **narrower**.

📌 **Example 7: Horizontal Stretch**  
Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = \left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)^2$.

* $g(x) = \left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)^2$ makes the parabola **wider**.
